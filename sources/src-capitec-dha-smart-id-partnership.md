## Summary

This is the closest working precedent in South Africa for the concept: a
regulated, identity-bound government service delivered inside bank branches,
with no government staff on site. Home Affairs and Capitec launched Smart ID and
passport applications through Capitec branches in August 2025. The design is the
interesting part. Home Affairs supplies an API and biometric verification;
Capitec supplies the premises, the self-service terminal and the queue; nobody
from the department is stationed in the branch. By mid-2026, 127 Capitec
branches were live, including rural districts such as Mankweng and Mokopane, out
of 259 bank branches nationally offering the service. If a bank branch can be
trusted to enrol biometrics for a national identity document, the question for
medicine collection stops being whether banks can host a regulated handover and
becomes which regulator has to say yes.

## Key claims

- On **11 August 2025** the Minister of Home Affairs announced **Capitec and FNB**
  as the inaugural participants in the department's digital partnership
  programme, enabling Smart ID and passport applications via bank branches and,
  later, the bank's mobile app.
- The model **does not station Home Affairs employees in bank branches**. The
  department is building an **API** that lets banks connect to the Home Affairs
  backend for application initiation, biometric enrolment and document
  collection.
- **Home Affairs biometric verification secures the process**; the bank supplies
  the physical channel.
- Applications run through **dedicated in-branch self-service terminals**, no
  prior booking, described as a **paperless process taking under five minutes**.
- Capitec's rollout was **phased**: an initial 10 branches, then a target of
  **100 branches by mid-2026** and **300 branches by end-2026**.
- By mid-2026, **127 Capitec branches** offered Smart ID applications, including
  rural districts such as **Mankweng and Mokopane in Limpopo**, alongside urban
  and peri-urban sites.
- Nationally, **259 bank branches** across participating banks offered the
  service, with Home Affairs targeting **750 bank branches by end-2026**.
- **Over 250,000 people** had used bank-facilitated applications for a
  replacement Smart ID since launch.
- Provincial spread noted for the Capitec Smart ID sites: Free State and North
  West five branches each (both previously had none under the older eHomeAffairs
  programme), Limpopo seven, Mpumalanga four, Northern Cape one, Gauteng 18
  (about 23.6% of the then-total). Capitec's spread was described as better
  aligned to provincial population distribution than the earlier programme.
