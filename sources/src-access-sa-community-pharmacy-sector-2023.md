## Summary

The most recent peer-reviewed count of the South African community pharmacy estate, and the source of the headline denominator for any access-gap arithmetic. Drawing on 2021 SAPC records, the paper puts roughly 3,580 registered community pharmacies in the country, of which 1,110 (31%) are corporate chain outlets sited in cities and towns. Approximately 1,253, or 35% of the national total, sit in Gauteng alone. The count is highest in Gauteng and lowest in Northern Cape.

Against a Census 2022 population of about 62 million, 3,580 community pharmacies works out at roughly one pharmacy per 17,300 people, or about 0.58 per 10,000. That is the number to hold next to the FIP global average in [[src-access-fip-community-pharmacy-2021]].

The paper is explicit that pharmacy siting tracks population density and economic activity rather than health need, and that rural areas have not seen the expansion urban areas have. Its own argument, that community pharmacies are an under-used delivery channel for HIV services, is the same structural argument a collection-point concept makes in reverse: where the pharmacy estate is thin, some other node has to carry the service.

## Key claims

- Approximately 3,580 community pharmacies were registered in South Africa according to 2021 SAPC records.
- 1,110 of those (31%) are corporate chain pharmacies located in cities and towns.
- The remainder are individually owned, many in less populated poorer settings.
- Approximately 1,253 registered community pharmacies (35% of the national total) are in Gauteng.
- Community pharmacy numbers are highest in Gauteng, the most populous province, and lowest in Northern Cape, the least populous.
- Geographical distribution of community pharmacies aligns with population occupancy and economic contribution rather than health need.
- Rural areas, generally poorer and less populated, have not seen the same pharmacy expansion as urban settings.
- Derived, not stated in the paper: 3,580 pharmacies against a Census 2022 population of ~62 million gives roughly one community pharmacy per 17,300 people (about 0.58 per 10,000).
