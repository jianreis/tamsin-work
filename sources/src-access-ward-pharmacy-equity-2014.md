## Summary

The anchor study for the access-gap question. Ward and colleagues took the South African Pharmacy Council register and measured community pharmacy density (pharmacies per 10,000 residents) across all nine provinces and 15 selected districts, comparing 1994 with 2012, then set that against public clinic density and a national benchmark of one clinic per 10,000 residents.

The finding that matters for a collection-point concept: pharmacy supply did not follow need. Pharmacy numbers grew 13% over eighteen years while the population grew 25%, so density fell. In 2012 density was eight times higher in the least deprived districts than in the most deprived. Only two of nine provinces met the 1-per-10,000 benchmark on community pharmacies alone. All nine met it only once public clinics were counted alongside pharmacies, which is the quantified version of the claim that for most South Africans the state clinic, not the retail pharmacy, is the medicine access point.

The gap between rural and urban provinces narrowed on one measure (the density difference between the most and least rural provinces fell from 1.3 to 0.72 per 10,000 between 1994 and 2012), but that convergence came from urban density falling rather than rural density rising.

## Key claims

- Community pharmacies in South Africa increased in number by 13% between 1994 and 2012, against 25% population growth over the same period. Density therefore declined.
- In 2012 community pharmacy density was eight times higher in the least deprived districts than in the most deprived districts.
- In 2012 only two of the nine provinces met a benchmark of one community pharmacy per 10,000 residents.
- All nine provinces met the 1-per-10,000 benchmark when community pharmacies and public clinics were counted together.
- The density difference between the most rural and least rural provinces fell from 1.3 community pharmacies per 10,000 residents to 0.72 per 10,000 between 1994 and 2012.
- Maldistribution persisted despite growth in corporate community pharmacy after the 2003 ownership deregulation.
- The authors record expert concern that absent rural incentives, unsuitable licensing criteria and pharmacy workforce shortage would undermine pharmaceutical access in rural areas under NHI.
- Study design: SAPC register analysis, nine provinces plus 15 selected districts, 1994 vs 2012, with public clinic density calculated alone and combined with community pharmacies.
