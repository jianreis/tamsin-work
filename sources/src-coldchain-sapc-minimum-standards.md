## Summary

In January 2017 SAPC approved five sets of minimum standards under the Good
Pharmacy Practice rules. Two of them are the regulatory spine for any
locker concept. Rule 2.3.5.5(i) covers procurement, storage and distribution of
thermolabile products, and defines thermolabile broadly as anything needing
constant cold storage below room temperature. Rule 1.10 covers a **remote
automated dispensing unit (RADU)**, which is the closest thing South African
pharmacy law already has to a medicine locker.

The RADU standard is written for an **institutional public pharmacy**. That is
the detail with consequences: the existing legal pattern for a machine handing
medicine to a patient away from a counter sits in the public sector, not in retail
community pharmacy. Anyone proposing a retail locker rail has to work out whether
it lands inside Rule 1.10, inside the mobile-unit standard (Rule 1.4), or in a gap
that needs SAPC engagement.

## Key claims

- Five minimum standards were approved for implementation in January 2017:
  mobile units (Rule 1.4); pharmacy websites (Rule 1.5); control of access to
  pharmacy premises (Rule 1.2.4(a)); procurement, storage and distribution of
  **thermolabile pharmaceutical products (Rule 2.3.5.5(i))**; and institutional
  public pharmacy operating a **remote automated dispensing unit, RADU (Rule 1.10)**.
- Thermolabile pharmaceutical products are defined as **all products which require
  constant cold storage at product-specific temperatures below room temperature**.
- All pharmacists are responsible for the effective, efficient and safe handling,
  storage and distribution of thermolabile products.
- Thermolabile products must be identified on receipt, stored per written
  instructions, with **temperatures monitored and recorded twice daily**.
- Controlled-temperature storage areas must be equipped with **temperature
  recorders** maintaining the whole area within range.
- Vaccines are normally stored between **2°C and not exceeding 8°C**.
- The RADU standard applies to **institutional public pharmacy**. No equivalent
  community-pharmacy RADU standard surfaced in the 2017 set.
