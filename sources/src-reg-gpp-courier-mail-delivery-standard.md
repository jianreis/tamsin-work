# GPP: supplying medicine by mail or courier, and to a person receiving on the patient's behalf

## Summary

This is the closest thing in South African law to permission for the locker
concept, and it is closer than expected. GPP contains a standard that expressly
contemplates a pharmacist selling, supplying or delivering medicine **by or
through the mail or a courier**, and expressly contemplates that the recipient
may be **a person who receives medicine on behalf of the patient**. The
conditions it imposes are logistical and informational: packaging that protects
safety, quality and efficacy through the whole delivery process, a control
system that detects and corrects delays, a report-back system for distribution
problems, and a patient information leaflet where one is available.

What the standard does not do is as important as what it does. It does not
require a pharmacist to be present when the patient takes the parcel. It does
not require the medicine to be handed over by a registered person. It does not
require counselling at the point of receipt; the informational obligation it
names is a leaflet. Read straight, GPP already accepts that dispensed medicine
can lawfully reach a patient through hands that are not a pharmacist's, provided
the dispensing pharmacy retains control and responsibility. A locker is a
courier leg with a machine at the end of it. Whether SAPC reads it that way is
the unresolved part, because the standard is written for mail and courier and
says nothing about an unattended holding facility that stores scheduled
medicine at a third party's premises for days.

## Key claims

- GPP expressly permits medicine or scheduled substances to be **sold, supplied
  or delivered by or through the mail or courier**, to a patient, to members of
  the public, **or to a person who receives medicine on behalf of the patient**.
- Four conditions attach, and all four sit with the pharmacist: packaging that
  guarantees safety, quality and efficacy in line with the medicine's
  registration requirements **throughout the delivery process**; a control system
  able to detect and correct delivery delays; a report-back system so
  distribution problems are detected timeously; and a patient information leaflet
  where available.
- The standard imposes **no requirement of pharmacist presence at the point of
  receipt**, and **no requirement of face-to-face counselling** at that point.
- Responsibility does not transfer with the parcel. The pharmacist, and any
  registered person assisting in the dispensing process within their scope,
  carries the professional duty to exercise proper and reasonable care and
  control over the medicine, per the Rules relating to the Code of Conduct made
  under s35A(b)(i) of the Pharmacy Act.
- GPP also contemplates a **locked prescription receiving box accessible only to
  the pharmacist** as a route for prescriptions to reach the pharmacy, which is
  the mirror-image of a locker and shows the rules do accommodate unattended
  physical infrastructure where access control is pharmacist-held.
- Nothing in the retrieved text addresses storage of dispensed scheduled medicine
  at a non-pharmacy site pending collection, which is the specific gap the bank
  branch locker sits in.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "When it is necessary to sell, supply or deliver medicine or scheduled
> substances by or through the mail/courier to a patient or to members of the
> public or to a person who receives medicine on behalf of the patient, the
> pharmacist must ensure that: (a) the medicine is packaged in such a manner that
> it will guarantee the safety, quality and efficacy of medicines in accordance
> with the registration requirements for such medicine throughout the delivery
> process; (b) a control system is implemented that will enable the pharmacist to
> detect and correct a delay in the delivery process; (c) a report back system is
> introduced to ensure that problems with medicine distribution and delivery are
> detected timeously; (d) where available a patient information leaflet is
> provided."

> "A pharmacist, and any other registered person who may assist in the dispensing
> process in line with the scope of practice for which they are registered, has
> the professional responsibility to exercise proper and/or reasonable care in
> respect of and control over medicines, in accordance with the Rules relating to
> the Code of Conduct for pharmacists and other persons registered in terms of
> the Pharmacy Act."

> "Prescriptions can be submitted to the pharmacy by electronic transmission,
> postal/courier delivery, or deposited in a locked prescription receiving box
> which is only accessible to the pharmacist."
