# The South African automated model that kept the pharmacist in the loop

## Summary

Africa's first Pharmacy Dispensing Unit, built by Right to Care and Right
ePharmacy with the Gauteng Department of Health, dispenses like an ATM but does
not remove the pharmacist. The patient scans an ID book, ID card or pharmacy card
and enters a PIN, then **talks to a remote pharmacist over an audio-video link**
before the medicine is robotically selected, labelled and dropped into a
collection slot. Five sites and 18 units were reported across Soweto,
Diepsloot, Alexandra and Bloemfontein, with waiting times cut to under three
minutes.

The contrast with a passive locker is the finding. South Africa's flagship
automated dispensing deployment chose to keep a live pharmacist at the point of
handover, by video, rather than to remove them. Whether that was a regulatory
requirement or a design choice is not stated in anything retrieved here, and that
distinction is exactly what a SAPC ruling would have to settle. Either way it
sets an expectation about what "acceptable" automation looks like locally, and a
PIN-only bank locker with a WhatsApp number sits on the other side of it.

Note the split in the same operator's portfolio: the PDU dispenses at the point
of contact with a remote pharmacist, while Collect&Go lockers hold already
dispensed parcels with no pharmacist at all
([[src-reg-collect-and-go-smart-lockers]]).

## Key claims

- The **PDU is the first of its kind in Africa**, developed by Right to Care and
  Right ePharmacy with the **Gauteng Department of Health**.
- Flow: patient scans a bar-coded ID book, ID card or pharmacy card and enters a
  **PIN**; **the patient talks to a remote pharmacist**; the prescription or
  items are selected; medicine is **robotically dispensed, labelled and dropped
  into the collection slot**.
- The audio-video link between patient and pharmacist is explicitly framed as
  **telepharmacy**, supporting "live patient counselling".
- Reported scale: **5 sites, 18 PDUs** in Soweto, Diepsloot, Alexandra and
  Bloemfontein. Patient waiting times reported cut to **under 3 minutes**.
- Dispensing, labelling and counselling all happen at the point of contact, which
  makes the PDU a remote pharmacy rather than a collection point.
