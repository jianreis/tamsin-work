# Dispensary Managers Survey — the staff view of script-journey friction

## Summary

Internal **survey of Dis-Chem dispensary managers** on the in-store
dispensary workflow: distributed via Microsoft Forms to all **286 dispensary
managers, 24 Oct – 9 Nov 2025**, with **102 responses (36%)**. Mixed
quantitative and open-ended questions across 48 fields — intake channels,
queues, counselling, systems (Unisolv, Dashboard, V-Sign), stock, the
Adherence Centre relationship, and what patients complain about. This is the
**staff's view of their own friction plus their perceived customer
friction** — the frontline lens on the same journey the customer research
describes from the outside. Two artefacts archived in `sources/surveys/`:
the raw response workbook (`dispensary-managers-survey-raw-2025.xlsx` — note
row 1 is a test response; the report's n=102 excludes it) and the analysed
report deck (`dispensary-managers-survey-report-2025.pptx`, 21 Nov 2025,
28 slides).

This is the survey the in-store walkthrough board quotes as "Dispensary
Manager Survey" ([[src-dc-instore-walkthrough-2026]]) — the two artefacts
now close that citation loop.

**Reliability — B (company primary, self-report):** a strong-n internal
census of the frontline, but staff self-report — treat statements about
*customer* feelings as staff perception, and claims about other teams (the
Adherence Centre, doctors) as one side of a seam.

## Sample

Gauteng 43% · Western Cape 24% · KZN 14% · EC 6% · MP 4% · NW 3% · smaller
provinces 6 responses · 1 Namibia. Team sizes: 16% small (0–5), 40%
medium-small (6–10), 25% mid (11–15), 18% large (16+). Pharmacist + PBQ
near-universal; Pharmacist Assistants 59%, locums 52%, Virtual Coordinators
49% — "meaningful differences in workload support" across the estate.

## The report's six themes (priority-ordered)

1. **Manual data entry & system fragmentation** (High). 77% of stores enter
   the same patient/script information twice (Dashboard + Unisolv); profile
   search and script capture are the most-named time sinks; 40%+ describe
   systems as manual-first/fragmented. "Unisolv is a manual process…
   dispensing, reporting, error tracking, profile search, merging profiles."
2. **Queue & workflow delays** (High). 75% have clear signage yet 25% report
   customers still joining the wrong queue; ~30% name queue/flow delays a top
   pain; multi-queuing (Dispensary → Collections → Payment) inflates
   perceived wait. "There is clear signage, but it is often misinterpreted."
3. **Collections mismatches & parcel rework** (High). Collections is the
   highest-friction stage: 20% of stores cite parcel mismatches/missing
   items as major daily friction; on-the-spot rework (correcting packs,
   reversing scripts, reprinting labels) slows the counter; customers arrive
   before parcels are ready, often off misunderstood SMSes; Pick & Pack
   rollout is inconsistent, leaving hybrid workflows. Opportunities named in
   the report: real-time parcel readiness + clearer SMS/app messaging (QR or
   unique PIN), Adherence-Centre/store alignment, pre-verification checks,
   self-service lockers, curb-side pickup.
4. **Technical constraints** (Medium). ~30–40% mention slow/freezing
   systems; Wi-Fi drops and device failures force full workflow resets;
   "technical slowdowns often look like inactivity to customers".
5. **Counselling & waiting engagement** (Low). Counselling is valued but
   shortened under peak pressure; "silent waiting" for pharmacist sign-off
   drives frustration. "We try to counsel properly, but during peak times
   it's impossible."
6. **Customer understanding & instructions** (Low). ~1 in 4 stores say
   customers regularly join the wrong queue; layout (pillars, clutter)
   defeats signage; staff spend time redirecting.

**Key additional insights (slide 27):** silent waiting amplifies every other
pain; collections rework erodes trust in "ready" messages, making people come
in to check in person; dispensary layout drives hovering and informal queues;
workflow pressure — not capability — is what degrades counselling.

## Channel pain (raw Q: "Which channels give you the most pain points?", n=103)

Adherence Centre orders (PMM/DeliverD/Integration/App) **58** · WhatsApp
**35** · Email **34** · Physical **15**. The Adherence Centre seam dominates
the open text: agents dispense out-of-stock or discontinued items without
checking the store, process items patients asked to leave off, claim on
wrong medical-aid codes or ignore scheme responses, print at closing/peak
time, merge multiple prescribers into one script needing in-store rewrites,
and route orders to the wrong store ("meds dispensed at incorrect branch…
repeats prepared at home branch"). WhatsApp is treated by customers "as a
chat site" with instant-answer expectations (scripts sent "2 minutes away
from the store", or late at night "and expect it to be ready when the store
open"); emails arrive with no instructions or detail ("not much detail than
a customer name"), land in spam, or lag ~10 minutes.

## What staff say patients complain about (raw Q, n=103 — the perceived-customer-friction column)

Recurring, in rough order of frequency:
- **Waiting and queues** — the modal complaint, worst at peaks.
- **Adherence-Centre order errors** — items added that weren't wanted, items
  left out, wrong generics, out-of-stocks dispensed, "they never get it
  right"; corrected live at the collections counter.
- **Reminder-call inconsistency, in both directions** — "some people complain
  that they DO get called and it is irritating and other complain that they
  DONT"; months with no call or SMS; repeats silently not processed;
  stopped-but-still-active repeats triggering SMSes and unwanted dispensing
  ("I did not order this"); early-refill SMSes causing unnecessary trips.
- **Script-validity confusion** — "customers don't understand the six month
  concept, they want to see six repeats on the label"; "why must they get
  script every 6 months"; last-repeat/expiry not communicated until the
  counter.
- **Money** — "med aid funds being depleted", "co-payments, to get a new
  repeat", "levy is to high/incorrect", co-pays not discussed by the call
  centre, authorisation/benefit-routing errors, PMB registration assumed
  automatic when it isn't.
- **S5/S6 copy rules** — doctors email schedule 5/6 scripts to the patient
  rather than the pharmacy; stores must refuse copies and demand originals;
  enforcement differs by pharmacy ("no clarity… stores approach this
  differently"), and the refusing store absorbs the anger.
- **Scripts not loaded / lost** — emailed scripts never captured, "scripts
  not visible on app", new scripts lost after hand-in.
- **Stock-outs** — including items the doctor prescribed that are
  discontinued or long out of stock.

> often don't read the SMS to say click on the link."

> "At collections customers are not always happy with the call centre… they
> process medication the client doesn't want. Then we have to reverse the
> script and correct it. It really takes time and the line become longer."

> "Some people complain that they DO get called and it is irritating and
> other complain that they DONT."

> "Customers don't understand the six month concept, they want to see six
> repeats on the label."

> "Levy is to high/incorrect. I did not order this - due to levy (we assume).
> I don't use this anymore - all valid repeats are triggering the sms and
> dispensing - some items have been stopped but are still active repeats."

> prescriptions directly to the pharmacy but rather to the patient, that
> makes it difficult in rendering good service."

> expect the prescription to be filled"

> "They send it late at night and expect it to be ready when the store open.
