# A licensed private courier pharmacy already delivers to third-party collection points

## Summary

Medipost is a licensed South African courier pharmacy. Alongside delivery to any
address, it offers delivery to a **preferred collection point**, and its own FAQ
states that the person collecting a parcel must **produce their identity document
on receipt**. That is the private-sector analogue the concept needs: a
retail pharmacy dispensing centrally, moving the parcel through non-pharmacy
hands, and handing it over against an ID check at a place that is not a pharmacy.

The limits of the evidence should be stated plainly. This is a company page, not
a regulator's determination. It shows a compliance position taken by an operator
with a lot to lose, which is worth something, but it does not tell you what SAPC
has approved, whether the collection points hold scheduled medicine overnight, or
what happens to counselling. It also does not extend to an unattended locker: a
staffed collection counter has a human who can check an ID and refuse a handover,
and a PIN pad does not.

## Key claims

- Medipost offers **free delivery to any address in South Africa**, including the
  option to have a parcel delivered to a **preferred collection point**.
- **The person collecting the parcel must produce their identity document (ID) on
  receipt of the parcel.**
- The operator is a private, licensed courier pharmacy serving medical-scheme
  chronic beneficiaries, so the model runs on a **sale**, unlike CCMDD's free
  State issue.
- No statement was retrieved about pharmacist presence at the collection point,
  about counselling at handover, or about storage conditions and duration at the
  collection point.
