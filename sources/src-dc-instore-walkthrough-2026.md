# Current State Friction — In Store (walkthrough board)

## Summary

Researcher-led **observational walkthrough of the as-is in-store script
journey** at Dis-Chem, captured as a current-state friction board ("Current
State Friction: High level overview — In Store") on the Customer Modernisation
Omnichannel Scripting Board V2.1. The board maps the walk-in journey step by
step — Access Channel (mall → store → dispensary) → Order Medication (queue,
identification, submit script, place order, waiting) → Receive Medication →
Purchase Medication (till queue, check-out, pay), with Browse Front Shop as a
parallel track — and pins friction and positive cards to each step. Evidence
mixes direct observation, dispensary-manager voice (including a dispensary
manager survey), and customer intercepts (numbered Customer 2/3/5/7); two
cards quote HelloPeter reviews (D-tier signal, flagged below).

**Reliability — B (qualitative observational, justify):** company primary
research by a researcher walking the journey in-store, with named-role staff
quotes and on-the-spot customer quotes. Not a sampled study; treat frequency
words ("often", "many") as the researcher's field judgement. The two
HelloPeter-quoted cards inherit D-tier weight for the quoted anecdotes.

## Journey steps as captured (customer voice)

1. **Access channel:** "I stop at Dis-Chem on the way home" (mall) → "I
   navigate the store to find the Dispensary."
2. **Queue:** "I find the right queue and take my place in it. I wait until I
   am called."
3. **Identification:** "I go to the counter. I state my purpose and provide
   identification."
4. **Submit script:** "I hand over my physical script. If there are repeat
   items, I confirm if I wish to load all repeats at Dischem, or I'd rather
   have the script returned to me."
5. **Place order:** "I confirm the medication I require today." / "I confirm
   my preferred payment method (cash or medical aid)."
6. **Waiting:** "I wait whilst the pharmacist prepares my order."
7. **Receive medication:** "I receive instructions for usage of the
   medication" ⇄ "I sign for my order and wait as my medication is packaged."
   → "I receive my medication. I take note if there is an outstanding amount."
8. **Purchase medication:** "I navigate to the till point, and join the
   queue." → "I provide my basket of goods, including my medication order, and
   loyalty card" → "I pay for my basket of goods with my card, including my
   medication order."

## Key claims — friction

- **Queue confusion.** Customers are often unsure which queue to join and
  frequently end up in the wrong one; even with signage the difference between
  queues isn't clear to many.
- **Long queues and staffing challenges.** Loud complaints about long queues
  and slow service, linked to understaffing at weekends and month-end; multiple
  counters visible but few staffed reads as inefficiency; the wait is
  especially hard on customers who are ill or physically weak.
- **Profile lookup friction.** Lookup is inconsistent, slow and frustrating —
  duplicate surnames, changed surnames, varying ID requirements; delays,
  repeated questions, and sometimes customers unable to collect at all.
- **Scripts not accessible across stores.** Scripts are not visible across
  branches even when the customer has a profile; pharmacists must contact the
  original store where the script was filled.
- **Slow or opaque script processing.** Long waits during processing,
  especially for longer or more complex scripts; customers can see activity
  behind the counter but not what's causing the delay — "slow systems"
  perception, time feels wasted.
- **Delays while waiting for pharmacist sign-off.** Dispensary staff wait for
  a pharmacist to become available to check or approve; pharmacists are
  frequently called away; the wait comes without any explanation.
- **Co-payments and medical-aid issues.** Unexpected levies, incorrect
  benefits, and funds depleted without warning.
- **Script not loaded / missing.** Complaints that handed-in scripts are not
  loaded, lost, or not visible on the app.
- **Dispensing errors and omissions.** Complaints of receiving the wrong
  medication or dose (HelloPeter-quoted; D-tier anecdote).
- **Payment process adds extra waiting.** After collecting medication,
  customers queue again to pay, in queues most often longer than the
  dispensary's; the final step feels like the slowest and most inefficient.

## Key claims — what works (positives, same board)

- **Personal relationships over waiting.** Some customers willingly wait
  longer to be served by a specific pharmacist they trust.
- **Staff friendliness and familiarity.** Regulars feel recognised; staff
  greet them by name and ask about their wellbeing.
- **Pharmacists helpful and knowledgeable.** Valued for personalised advice,
  recommending cheaper or alternative options, even calling medical aids to
  resolve issues.
- **Pharmacist consultation adds meaningful value.** Time and care explaining
  medication use — demonstrations, practical advice — builds trust and
  confidence, contrasted with pharmacies where customers "figure it out"
  themselves.
