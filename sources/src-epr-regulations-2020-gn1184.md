## Summary

South Africa's mandatory packaging EPR regime. GN 1184 sets the framework and
GN 1187 (published alongside) is the section 18 notice for paper, packaging and
some single-use products. "Producer" is defined broadly and includes brand
owners; industry guidance treats a retailer as the producer for its house
brands and imported filled packaging. Producers must register with DFFE, join
or run a producer responsibility organisation (PRO), pay EPR fees and meet
collection and recycling targets. Any Dis-Chem-branded returnable pack is
Dis-Chem's EPR obligation. Direct fetch blocked on 2026-09-07; claims from
search extracts of LawLibrary, CER PDF, DFFE, Packaging SA, Petco and eWASA
explainers.

## Key claims

- Published 5 November 2020 (GN 1184); in effect 5 May 2021.
- "Producer" means any person or category of persons, including a brand owner,
  engaged in the commercial manufacture, conversion, refurbishment or import of
  new or used identified products.
- "Extended producer responsibility" extends a producer's responsibility for an
  identified product to the post-consumer stage of its life cycle.
- Purpose: framework for producers to develop, implement, monitor and evaluate
  EPR schemes and to enable circular-economy initiatives.
- Existing producers had to register with the department within six months of
  commencement; new producers within three months.
- Industry PRO guidance (Packaging SA, Petco): for packaging the producer is the
  brand owner, the retailer for house brands, or the importer of filled
  packaging; obligation applies above 10 tonnes per year (⚠️ threshold from PRO
  explainer, not confirmed in regulation text).
- Obligations include EPR fees per material, annual collection and recycling
  targets, life-cycle assessments and waste-picker integration (Packaging SA
  summary).
