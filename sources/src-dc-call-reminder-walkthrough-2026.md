# Current State Friction — Call Reminder (repeat-reminder walkthrough board)

## Summary

Researcher-led **observational walkthrough of the as-is repeat-reminder
("call reminder") journey** at Dis-Chem, captured as a current-state friction
board ("Current State Friction: High level overview — Call Reminder") on the
Customer Modernisation Omnichannel Scripting Board V2.1.1. The journey: a
Dis-Chem representative phones to offer to prepare repeat medication → the
customer confirms and names a collection store → they're given a
collect-from/collect-by window and follow-up confirmation (WhatsApp example
on the board: "Kindly note that an amount of R1151.89 will be payable on
collection. Click below to track your order.") → in-store collection (queue,
identification, waiting, receive) → till payment. Friction and positive cards
pin to each step; evidence mixes observation, dispensary-manager voice,
and HelloPeter reviews (D-tier, flagged). A pink board sticky records the
researcher's open item: "Add After buying friction: after payment - but WHY
was their a levy. Now need to piece stuff together myself."

**Reliability — B (qualitative observational, justify):** company primary
research; same basis and caveats as [[src-dc-instore-walkthrough-2026]]. The
HelloPeter-quoted cards inherit D-tier weight for the quoted anecdotes.

## Key claims — friction

- **Phone reminder experience.** Some customers dislike the repeat-reminder
  calls: bombarded with frequent calls, the service feels pushy rather than
  helpful. Agents may not clearly identify themselves, may jump straight into
  asking for personal details, or may be difficult to hear — raising scam
  concerns. Customers end up irritated, confused, or pressured instead of
  supported.
- **Repeat prescription service reliability.** Customers don't trust the
  repeat service to deliver correctly or reliably: even after signing up,
  medication doesn't arrive, forcing last-minute scrambles to fill scripts.
  The reminders happen, but the operational follow-through fails. The call
  centre and dispensary aren't in sync — calls arrive after new scripts were
  already submitted, or carry incorrect medication details — so the service
  reads as disorganised and even incompetent.
- **Unreliable and unclear communication.** After placing an order, SMS or
  WhatsApp messages are often not received, delayed, or misleading, and
  stores frequently don't know what the Adherence Centre has communicated.
  Customers arrive expecting medication to be ready — because they were told
  it would be — and find it not prepared, out of stock, or awaiting
  confirmation.
- **Confusing and frustrating queue.** Unclear signage and a poorly defined
  layout make the collections queue hard to find and distinguish; it overlaps
  with other lines and attracts customers who aren't there to collect. With
  the counter unmanned, people aren't sure how to get help or whether they're
  in the right place. The bell system partly mitigates this when noticed, but
  isn't available in every store.
- **Inconsistent and unreliable ID process.** Inconsistent ID checks and
  frequent profile mismatches: some staff search only by surname (unreliable
  when surnames are shared), others use the Dis-Chem card, app, or ID number;
  address discrepancies across systems add confusion. Customers now *expect*
  friction at this stage.
- **Packages not ready when customers arrive.** Customers arrive expecting
  the parcel to be ready — they sent the script, got an SMS, or assumed the
  pharmacy would process it — and are told the order wasn't filled or
  captured, forcing them to wait, return later, or rejoin the dispensary
  queue after believing they'd already "done their part".
- **Incorrect or unwanted items.** Packages are sometimes incorrect,
  incomplete, or include items the customer didn't request; staff fix orders
  on the spot — rewriting scripts, correcting repeats, reversing items —
  slowing the collections queue for everyone.
- **Long waits while staff search for the package.** Even when orders are
  ready, customers wait while staff hunt through storage; collecting for
  someone else or under a different surname slows it further. The "quick
  collection" feels slow, disorganised, and unpredictable.
- **Co-payments and medical-aid issues.** Unexpected levies, incorrect
  benefits, funds depleted without warning.
- **Payment process adds extra waiting.** Re-queueing to pay after
  collection, in queues most often longer than the dispensary's.
- **Researcher's open item (pink sticky):** after-payment friction — the levy
  is unexplained and the customer must "piece stuff together" themselves.

## Key claims — what works

- **Queues move quickly and feel efficient.** Despite appearing long at first
  glance, till queues generally move quickly once joined; friendly, efficient
  cashiers; the process is described as "easy" and "quick".
