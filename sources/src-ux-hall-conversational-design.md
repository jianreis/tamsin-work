# Summary

Hall's book argues that conversation is the oldest interface and that digital
systems should meet its norms rather than expect people to learn a machine's.
Her nine principles for conversational systems (derived from Grice's maxims)
are a compact checklist for any WhatsApp exchange: cooperative, goal-oriented,
context-aware, quick and clear, turn-based, truthful, polite, error-tolerant,
and "be a pal". Her working method also transfers directly: write the human
conversation first, with no interface, and let that script drive the design -
which is exactly what a WhatsApp mock is.

**Retrieval note:** the book itself was not fetched; principles captured from

# Key claims

- Nine principles of conversational systems: **cooperative, goal-oriented,
  context-aware, quick and clear, turn-based, truthful, polite,
  error-tolerant, be a pal**.
- **Turn-based**: an interaction is an exchange of turns; the system takes its
  turn, then yields. In chat terms, one turn does one job, then waits.
- **Quick and clear**: brevity is a courtesy, not a constraint; make the
  point, then stop.
- **Error-tolerant**: people will say things the system did not plan for;
  handling that gracefully is part of the design, not an edge case.
- Method: imagine and write the conversation you would have with the user if
  there were no interface, then use that conversation to guide the design.
- Systems should listen before speaking and maintain state across modes and
  devices - a returning customer should not have to re-establish context.
