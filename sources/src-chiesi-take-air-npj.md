## Summary

Peer-reviewed evaluation of "Take AIR", a 12-month Chiesi-funded postal
inhaler take-back run through community pharmacies in Leicester, Leicestershire
and Rutland (Feb 2021 to Feb 2022). Pharmacies handed patients pre-paid
Royal Mail envelopes; patients posted used inhalers of any brand to a waste
contractor, which dismantled pMDIs, recycled aluminium and plastic and
recovered propellant. It is the strongest evidence that a pharmacy-fronted,
patient-posted return loop for used medicine devices can work. Direct fetch
blocked on 2026-09-07; claims from search extracts of the Nature/PMC article and
Pharmaceutical Journal coverage.

## Key claims

- 20,049 inhalers returned over 12 months; 77% were pressurised metered-dose
  inhalers (pMDIs).
- Estimated 119.3 tonnes CO2-equivalent avoided, mostly from recovered
  propellant.
- Mechanism: pharmacist discusses scheme, gives pre-paid pre-addressed
  envelope; up to four inhalers per envelope; Royal Mail tracked post to the
  waste contractor.
- pMDIs dismantled and components recycled; residual propellant extracted for
  reuse in refrigeration and air-conditioning; other inhaler types went to
  energy-from-waste.
- 141 of 227 invited pharmacies (62%) signed up (Pharmaceutical Journal).
- Authors conclude the scheme is feasible and effective and a foundation for
  future initiatives; the pilot was later extended six months.
