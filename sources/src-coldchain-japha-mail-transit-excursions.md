## Summary

The strongest published evidence that ordinary parcel logistics does not hold
medicines inside their labelled storage band. Researchers shipped packages
carrying data loggers between New Jersey, California and Tennessee across a winter
and a summer window, then measured time spent outside the USP <659> controlled
room temperature range. The result is not marginal: packages averaged 68.3% of
transit time out of range, with a median of 80%.

This is transit, not a locker, and it is the United States, not South Africa. But
it establishes the base rate. If a monitored, professionally operated parcel
network cannot hold 20-25°C, an unmonitored steel box standing in a forecourt is
not going to do better by accident. It also shows why the honest question about
lockers is a measurement question rather than a belief question.

## Key claims

- Prospective study; packages with data-logging thermometers shipped between three
  US locations over winter (Dec 2019 - Feb 2020) and summer (Aug - Sep 2020).
- **35 packages spent an average of 68.3% of time out of range** relative to the
  USP <659> band of 68-77°F (20-25°C).
- Distribution skewed high: **median 80% of transit time out of range**.
- Results were analysed by shipping method, carrier and season; excursions occurred
  across all of them.
- A related figure reported from the same body of work: only **8.4% of packages**
  experienced variation within the excursions allowable under the USP definition
  of controlled room temperature.
