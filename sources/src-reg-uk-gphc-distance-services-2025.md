# NON-SA. The UK regulator's distance-services guidance, including prescription collection units

## Summary

GPhC's February 2025 guidance is the British regulator's standing position on
pharmacy at a distance, and it contains a section on **prescription collection
units**, which is the closest formal regulatory treatment of the locker question
found anywhere in this research. The guidance requires pharmacies to risk-assess
the service, keep accurate records, verify patient identity through secure
methods, and choose the consultation format (video, phone or in person) according
to the patient's needs and the risk profile of the medicine. For high-risk
medicines the prescriber may not rely on an online questionnaire alone and must
verify independently through timely two-way communication, the clinical record,
or the patient's GP.

Two principles are portable. Identity verification is treated as a named
obligation of the service, not an afterthought at the counter. And the choice of
counselling channel is **risk-tiered rather than fixed**, which is the doctrinal
move a South African locker proposal needs SAPC to make: not "remote counselling
is or is not allowed", but "which medicines and which patients can be served by
which channel".

Marked NON-SA.

## Key claims

- The guidance covers registered pharmacies providing services at a distance,
  including on the internet, and supplements GPhC's mandatory standards.
- It contains a section on **prescription collection units**. Text not retrieved.
- Pharmacies must conduct **risk assessments**, keep accurate records, and run
  governance focused on patient safety.
- Pharmacy professionals must **confirm patient identity through secure
  verification methods**.
- The **consultation format (video, phone or in person) must be chosen according
  to patient needs and medication risk**, rather than fixed by rule.
- For high-risk medicines (weight-management medicines, medicines needing
  physical examination) the prescriber **may not rely on an online questionnaire
  alone** and must verify independently through timely two-way communication with
  the person, their clinical records, or their GP or regular prescriber.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "For high-risk medicines - particularly medicines used for weight-management
> and medicines requiring physical examination before prescribing - the prescriber
> cannot base prescribing decisions on the information provided in an online
> questionnaire alone. Instead, the prescriber has to independently verify the
> information through timely two-way communication with the person, accessing
> their clinical records, or contacting their GP or regular prescriber."

> "Pharmacists must select an appropriate consultation format (video, phone, or
> in-person) based on patient needs and medication risks, and must confirm patient
> identity through secure verification methods."
