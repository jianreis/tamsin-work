## Summary

FIP's global community pharmacy survey, run November 2020 to January 2021, gives the international yardstick the South African numbers need. The global average is 2.78 community pharmacies per 10,000 population and 5.19 community pharmacists per 10,000, both up about 11 to 12% since the 2016 round.

Put South Africa's roughly 0.58 community pharmacies per 10,000 ([[src-access-sa-community-pharmacy-sector-2023]]) against that global average and the country sits at roughly a fifth of the world mean, in the band FIP associates with low-income countries, which it says have fewer than one pharmacy per 10,000. South Africa is an upper-middle-income country with a retail pharmacy density typical of a low-income one. That comparison is the single cleanest way to state the size of the access gap to a non-specialist audience.

FIP also notes that in the African and South-East Asian regions the ratio of pharmacists to pharmacies falls below one, implying some pharmacies operate without a pharmacist present. That constrains what a pharmacy-shaped solution can do and pushes toward models where pharmacist oversight is centralised and only the handover is distributed.

Reliability is B rather than A: FIP is the global professional federation and the report is official, but the underlying data are self-reported by member organisations with uneven country coverage.

## Key claims

- Global average community pharmacy density: 2.78 community pharmacies per 10,000 population.
- Global average community pharmacist density: 5.19 community pharmacists per 10,000 population.
- Community pharmacist density rose 12.1% and community pharmacy density rose 11.2% since the 2016 round.
- Low-income countries have fewer than one community pharmacy per 10,000 population.
- In the African and South-East Asian regions the ratio of pharmacists to pharmacies is below one, indicating some pharmacies operate without a pharmacist.
- Wider workforce data: sample mean of 6 pharmacists per 10,000 population, range 0.02 (Somalia) to 25.07 (Malta).
- Pharmacist density correlates with gross national income and health expenditure.
- Survey fieldwork ran November 2020 to January 2021.
