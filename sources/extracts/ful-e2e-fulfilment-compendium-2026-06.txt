Current state script fulfilment offers a basis to build from, with further automation, efficiency, and integration providing the opportunity to process scripts in ~19 min

Internal interviews
Duplication is reduced but still exists (Pharmacists verify in AC & Dispensary) Training program in place to scale PBQs for volume growth, but high initial expect growth requires increasing net PBQ count Current team set up allows for transition to MLP and future states
OCRs are in use today but have low accuracy rates (~30%) Script processing requires switching across multiple dashboards adding complexity, time, and information loss (notes, pharmacist validations in AC)
Uses central script processing (AC) and fulfilment (DC for uncommon items) GP integration in place but is not source of most scripts; GPs are skeptical to issue e-scripts Stock visibility is available but occasionally inaccurate, requiring calls to dispensaries to check
Global benchmarks
Pharmacist to PBQ ratios are similar across regions (~1:3 in SA, Brazil, US, etc.) and are dictated by legislation
Higher-accuracy OCRs help process scripts quickly (Tata 1mg has <1-min prescription validation) Shorter end-to-end processing times with streamlined decisioning and use of automation (Apollo processes and delivers in 19-min)
Central script processing is present across the board (Farmatodo, Pague Menos, Tata 1mg, Apollo) In-house GPs generate larger share of scripts (Pague Menos, Apollo) Large investments in stock accuracy to increase efficiency (Farmatodo has live inventory software)
Summary of findings
How this informs target state
Global benchmarks indicate that scripts can be processed in ~19 min, therefore, a 30 min customer promise is feasible but is dependent on: High script and pick and pack automation efficiencies which can significantly reduce script processing SLAs (e.g., Apollo 247 delivers an order in 19-minutes) E2E enablers such as a singular, end-to-end dashboard, increased stock visibility and order management will streamline order processing and reduce information loss (e.g., Brix)
Source: Dis-Chem interviews, expert interviews, press search
