# NON-SA. A written rule for exactly this machine, and what it demands

## Summary

North Carolina has drafted the rule South Africa has not. A direct-to-patient
delivery system is permitted, and the conditions are specific enough to be
lifted almost wholesale into a design brief. The system must **convey the home
pharmacy's offer to counsel** and must provide the ability for the patient to
have an **immediate real-time consultation with a licensed pharmacist employed by
the home pharmacy**. Where the dispensing pharmacist decided the patient needs
counselling, the system must be able to **force counselling before it releases
the medicine**. The communication link must protect confidentiality, must be
checked at least daily, and **the system must close if the link fails or if no
pharmacist is available** for counselling, unless a pharmacist is physically
present at the unit. One pharmacist may cover no more than three sites at once.

The regulatory logic is worth naming: North Carolina did not require presence, it
required **availability, immediacy and a fail-closed link**. Counselling access
substitutes for counselling in person, but only if the access is real-time and
the machine refuses to dispense when it is not. A WhatsApp number on a label
would not meet that bar. A locker that stays open when the pharmacist line is
down would fail it outright.

Marked NON-SA. Useful as a design template and as evidence that the problem is
solvable by rule, not as authority in South Africa.

## Key claims

- The DTP system **shall convey the home pharmacy's offer to counsel** the
  patient.
- It **shall provide the ability for an immediate real-time consultation** with a
  pharmacist licensed by the Board and employed by the home pharmacy.
- Where the dispensing pharmacist determined counselling is needed, the system
  **shall be able to force counselling before it dispenses**.
- The communication link **shall protect the confidentiality** of patient
  information and **shall be checked at least daily** by the home pharmacy.
- The system **shall be closed if the link malfunctions or if no licensed
  pharmacist is available** for counselling, unless a pharmacist is physically
  present at the unit. Fail-closed, not fail-open.
- A counselling pharmacist may not cover **more than three sites
  simultaneously**.
- Sibling rules in the same chapter cover receiving and dispensing (21 NCAC 46
  .1804) and patient counselling (21 NCAC 46 .2504).
- Broader US position, per practitioner commentary: states vary; many permit
  remote or telepharmacy counselling under defined conditions, some require
  real-time audio-video, documented refusal, or in-person consultation for
  particular drug classes.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "The DTP system shall convey the home pharmacy's offer to counsel a patient and
> shall provide the ability for the patient to have an immediate real-time
> consultation with a pharmacist licensed by the Board and employed by the home
> pharmacy."

> "If the dispensing pharmacist has determined that the patient should receive
> counseling before the prescription is dispensed, the DTP system shall provide
> the ability for the pharmacist to force counseling before the DTP system
> dispenses the drug, device, or medical equipment."

> "The communication link shall protect the confidentiality of the patient's
> information. The home pharmacy shall check the communication link at least
> daily and the DTP system shall be closed if the link malfunctions or if a
> licensed pharmacist is not available from the home pharmacy for counseling,
> unless a licensed pharmacist is physically present at the DTP system."

> "A pharmacist who is responsible for counseling may not provide that service for
> more than three sites simultaneously."
