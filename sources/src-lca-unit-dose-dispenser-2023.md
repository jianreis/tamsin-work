## Summary

The only LCA found that compares single-use unit-dose packaging against
reusable medication dispensers, in a hospital closed-loop medication
administration setting. Two of three reusable-dispenser scenarios carried a
higher environmental burden than the unit-dose equivalent unless the dispenser
was reused at least about 7.2 times. The authors note no prior LCA of unit-dose
medication packaging existed. It is the closest evidence base for the
"returnable dispenser beats disposable sachet" intuition: true, but only past a
reuse threshold. Direct fetch blocked on 2026-09-07; claims from search
extracts of the Springer abstract and repository record.

## Key claims

- Scope: pharmaceutical packaging (blisters, bottles) and clinical packaging
  (unit doses vs reusable medical dispensers) in closed-loop medication
  administration; cradle-to-grave model.
- Two of three dispenser scenarios showed higher environmental burden than the
  respective unit-dose scenario; the trend reverses only if the dispenser is
  reused 7.2 times or more.
- States that no LCA of unit-dose medication packaging had been published
  before this study.
- Setting is hospital ward practice, not community pharmacy home delivery;
  transfer to consumer sachets is an inference.
