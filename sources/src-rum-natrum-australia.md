## Summary

Australia's Commonwealth-funded national medicine take-back: every pharmacy has
a RUM bin, the public returns unwanted or expired medicine free, and everything
returned is incinerated. The programme's own FAQ is explicit that nothing
returned is reused or recycled. It is the clearest official statement in a
comparable jurisdiction that a pack which has left the pharmacy with medicine in
it comes back only for destruction. Direct fetch blocked on 2026-09-07; claims
from search extracts of returnmed.com.au and health.gov.au.

## Key claims

- National not-for-profit funded by the Commonwealth through the Department of
  Health; grant of AUD 22,498,300 (GST incl.) for 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2027.
- Any pharmacy in Australia accepts unwanted or out-of-date medicines free of
  charge.
- Returned medicines are not reused or recycled by the pharmacy at any time;
  disposal contractors incinerate at high temperature under EPA requirements,
  without further inspection.
- Cumulative collection: over 12 million kg of unwanted medicine since the
  project began (RUM figure, ⚠️ single-source).
- RUM points customers to Pharmacycle bins for empty blister packs, which are a
  separate recycling stream.

## Verbatim excerpts
