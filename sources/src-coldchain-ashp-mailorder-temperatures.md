## Summary

An independent replication of the mail-transit excursion problem, on a larger
package count and across six climates. Forty-eight non-refrigerated packages
carrying data loggers were mailed to Baltimore, Chicago, Tucson, Palo Alto, Largo
and Katy in a winter and a summer window. Every single shipment went out of range
at some point. Winter was worse than summer, which is a useful corrective to the
assumption that heat is the only threat: cold excursions damage products too, and
some formulations are destroyed by freezing.

The relevant transfer to a South African locker rail is the failure rate, not the
absolute temperatures. Out-of-range was the normal condition, not the exception.

## Key claims

- **48 non-refrigerated packages** with temperature data loggers were shipped
  through the US Postal Service to **six US cities** across Dec 2019 - Feb 2020 and
  Jun - Aug 2020.
- **Every shipment** was exposed to temperatures outside the recommended range at
  some point during transit.
- Time out of range: **68% to 87% in winter**, **27% to 54% in summer**.
- Reference band used: 68-77°F (20-25°C), the USP controlled room temperature range.
- Products exposed outside the recommended range can be chemically or physically
  altered and lose effectiveness.
