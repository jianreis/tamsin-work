## Summary

USP <659> and the USP General Notices define the temperature bands that almost
every pharmaceutical storage instruction in the world is written against, South
Africa included. The numbers matter for a locker because they set out how much
heat a shelf-stable product is actually allowed to see, and the answer is less
generous than the phrase "room temperature" suggests. Controlled room temperature
is 20-25°C. Brief excursions to 15-30°C are tolerated only if the mean kinetic
temperature stays at or below 25°C. Transient spikes to 40°C are allowed for up to
24 hours.

Two design consequences. A shelf-stable rail is not a rail with no temperature
requirement, it is a rail with a 25°C mean kinetic ceiling. And an unattended box
in South African sun can breach 30°C for far longer than "brief" without anyone
knowing, unless it is monitored.

## Key claims

- **Controlled room temperature (CRT)**: 20-25°C, the temperature maintained
  thermostatically that encompasses the usual working environment.
- **Permitted excursions**: 15-30°C, provided mean kinetic temperature (MKT) stays
  at or below 25°C.
- **Transient spikes**: up to 40°C are permitted for up to 24 hours where MKT
  remains acceptable and exposure is minimised.
- **Mean kinetic temperature** expresses the overall effect of temperature
  fluctuation across storage or transit; it should typically not exceed 25°C.
- CRT is therefore a time-weighted thermal exposure limit, not a momentary reading.
- Cold chain / refrigerated storage is **2-8°C**; ambient or "cool" storage bands
  are commonly cited as 15-25°C in dispensing guidance.
