## Summary

The closest published South African measurement of what happens inside an
uninsulated steel enclosure standing in the sun. Researchers logged indoor
temperature for 14 days inside a seven-storey shipping-container block in
Johannesburg. Indoor apparent temperature ran **7°C higher than outdoor on
average**, peaking at **42°C at 17:00**, and insulation made a difference of 2-9°C.

Johannesburg is one of the mildest large-city climates in South Africa. The same
enclosure in Upington, Musina or Nelspruit would run hotter, and a small
sealed locker compartment has a worse surface-area-to-volume ratio than a room.
The finding does not measure a parcel locker, so it is a proxy rather than a direct
answer, but it is a South African proxy with real instrumentation, which is more
than the locker vendors publish.

For a shelf-stable rail this puts the USP mean-kinetic-temperature ceiling of 25°C
in play: an unconditioned steel locker in a Highveld forecourt would plausibly
breach 30°C daily through summer afternoons.

## Key claims

- Cross-sectional study; **indoor temperature measured for 14 days** inside a
  seven-storey shipping-container block of apartments and businesses in Johannesburg.
- **Median indoor apparent temperature 16°C**, range **6°C (at 08:00) to 42°C (at
  17:00)**.
- **Indoor temperature averaged 7°C higher than outdoor temperature.**
- Temperature differences between insulated and uninsulated container buildings
  ranged **2°C to 9°C**.
- Occupant-reported heat effects: headaches (58%), tiredness or weakness (40%).
- Recommendation: insulation, adequate windows and ventilation or air conditioning
  for container structures in hot climates.
- This measures an occupied building, not a sealed parcel locker. Applying it to a
  locker is an inference. `⚠️ UNVERIFIED` as a locker measurement.
