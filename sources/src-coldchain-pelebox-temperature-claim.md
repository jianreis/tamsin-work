## Summary

Pelebox is the other South African medicine-locker precedent, and its own product
page makes a specific regulatory claim: the locker is temperature controlled to
meet standards prescribed by the South African Pharmacy Council. That claim is
worth taking seriously and worth verifying, because it is the closest thing to a
public statement that an SA locker vendor is designing against SAPC storage rules.

What it does not say is which standard, what band, whether performance is logged,
or whether the fleet has ever been audited against it. It also does not claim
cold-chain capability. Pelebox carries CCMDD-style chronic parcels, and it is sited
inside clinics and community buildings such as libraries rather than in open
forecourts, which does a lot of the thermal work on its own.

Reliability C: vendor self-description, corroborated on scope but not on
temperature performance by provincial health and media reporting.

## Key claims

- "The locker is temperature controlled to meet standards prescribed by the South
  African Pharmacy Council." Vendor claim; no band, no logging protocol, no audit
  evidence published. `⚠️ UNVERIFIED` as to substantiation.
- Stores **pre-packed repeat chronic medication**; SMS one-time PIN plus cellphone
  or ID number opens the compartment. No app, no data needed by the patient.
- Sited **inside a clinic** as a speed-collection service, or at a community
  building such as a library closer to the patient.
- Used in conjunction with **CCMDD** parcels; 30 lockers across KwaZulu-Natal since
  the first in 2019 (Health Systems Trust partnership); support for patients in
  over 120 communities.
- No refrigerated compartment or cold-chain line is described.
