## Summary

The US regulators' association position on whether dispensed medicine can come
back into stock. The answer is: only if it never left the pharmacy's control.
"Returned and reused" is defined narrowly as medicine taken out for delivery by
pharmacy staff, a contracted courier or approved carrier, and brought back
undelivered or refused, with custody unbroken throughout. Anything that reached
a patient is out. Controlled substances are excluded entirely. Useful as the
international norm SA practice mirrors. Direct fetch blocked on 2026-09-07;
claims from search extracts of the NABP PDF and news release.

## Key claims

- Boards of pharmacy have concerns about the quality and integrity of drugs
  previously dispensed and returned by patients for reuse.
- NABP could endorse return-and-reuse programmes only where a safe protocol for
  accepting and processing medications is demonstrated.
- "Returned and reused medications" are limited to items removed for delivery
  and returned because they were undeliverable or refused, and which never left
  the control of pharmacy staff, the contracted delivery service or approved
  carrier.
- Controlled substances may not be included in any return-and-reuse programme.
- The FDA's Compliance Policy Guide 460.300 separately records injuries from
  drugs returned by patrons and resold by pharmacists.
