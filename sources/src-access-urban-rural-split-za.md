## Summary

Filed to replace the vendor-blog figure of ">35.7% rural" that the vault currently carries as unverified in [[wiki/consumer-context/last-mile-addressing-logistics]] and [[src-con-pargo-pudo-lastmile]].

The World Bank series puts South Africa at 69.3% urban in 2024, implying roughly 30.7% rural. For 2022, the year of the census, the same series gives about 68.3% urban and 31.7% rural. Both are materially below the 35.7% the vendor blog asserts. The vendor figure is not obviously wrong so much as undated and unsourced, and it may reflect an older year or a different settlement-type definition.

Two honest limits. First, this is a World Bank series built on UN World Urbanization Prospects estimates, not a Stats SA count, which is why it sits at B rather than A. Second, Stats SA Census 2022 does capture geography type (urban/rural) as a variable, but the headline national percentage was not obtainable in this session (statssa.gov.za is blocked by the egress proxy, and the figure does not appear in the indexed media release). The authoritative South African split therefore remains unretrieved. Anyone integrating this should pull the Census 2022 statistical release or GHS 2023 (P0318) directly and supersede this note.

Practical guidance for the vault: use "roughly a third of South Africans live in rural areas" and cite this note, rather than the precise ">35.7%". The concept does not turn on the second decimal, and the current figure cannot be defended.

## Key claims

- Urban population as a share of total was 69.3% in South Africa in 2024 (World Bank WDI), implying approximately 30.7% rural.
- For 2022 the same series gives approximately 68.3% urban, implying approximately 31.7% rural.
- Urban population was reported at 44,355,700 in 2024.
- Urban population growth was 1.94% annually in 2024.
- Census 2022 (Stats SA, released 10 October 2023) counted approximately 62 million people in South Africa.
- Census 2022 includes geographic type (urban/rural) among its geography variables, but the national headline percentage was not obtainable in this session.
- The ">35.7% rural" figure carried in the vault from a vendor blog is higher than the World Bank series for any recent year and remains unsourced to a primary count.
