## Summary

The operating model behind the shelf-stable-only rule. CCMDD pre-dispenses repeat
chronic medicine centrally and pushes it to collection points closer to where
people live: contracted external pick-up points including independent pharmacies
and retail sites, community adherence clubs, and fast lanes inside health
facilities. Roughly two-thirds of collections now happen at external pick-up
points, and waiting time there is under ten minutes for almost everyone surveyed.

What the model does not include is refrigerated capacity at the collection end.
The parcels are ambient-storage boxes handed over at a counter. There is no
published fridge specification for an external pick-up point, and insulin is
excluded from the programme entirely.

## Key claims

- CCMDD began in February 2014 as a decentralised chronic-medicine delivery model
  for public-sector patients.
- Reported enrolment: **5.3 million patients registered, 2.9 million active**; a
  separate figure cites 4.9 million patients served. Figures vary by source and
  date; treat as directional.
- **64% of patients collect at external pick-up points**, not at a facility.
- Pick-up point types: contracted **external PuPs** (locations outside public health
  facilities, including independent pharmacies), **community-based adherence clubs**,
  and **fast lanes** at registered health care facilities.
- **99% of 954 surveyed patients** were assisted within 10 minutes at a PuP;
  average wait under 10 minutes.
- Eligibility: over 18, able to consent, clinically stable, on the same regimen for
  at least 12 months with two most recent laboratory results normal. Patients on TB
  treatment or with conditions needing regular consultation are excluded. No acute
  medicines are on the CCMDD formulary.
- No refrigerated storage specification for external pick-up points was found in
  the programme material. `⚠️ UNVERIFIED` - absence of a published specification,
  not a documented prohibition.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "External pick-up points (PuPs) provide patients with a more convenient option for
> the collection of their repeat medicine which has been dispensed and distributed
> via the programme, and the PuP may be closer to their homes or workplace and can
> result in reduced transport costs."

> "PuPs include contracted pick-up points (external PuP) in locations external to
> public health facilities, including independent pharmacies; community-based
> outreach clubs or adherence clubs; and fast lanes which are based at registered
> health care facilities."

> "Patients are eligible for the CCMDD programme if they are older than 18 years of
> age, able to provide consent and are stable on chronic medication, which is
> defined as being on the same treatment regimen for at least 12 months, with the
> two most recent laboratory results being normal."
