## Summary

Shoprite's own announcement of the Sixty60 bag take-back: customers hand used
paper delivery bags to the Sixty60 driver at the next delivery and the store
recycles them. It is a recycling loop, not a reuse loop - the bags are pulped,
not refilled. It is the closest SA precedent for a driver-collected return leg
on a home-delivery service. Direct fetch was blocked by the network egress
proxy on 2026-09-07; claims are drawn from search extracts of the Shoprite
newsroom page and three corroborating trade-press reposts of the same release.

## Key claims

- Sixty60 customers can return used paper delivery bags to Checkers via the
  Sixty60 driver at their next delivery; the driver takes them back to the store
  for recycling.
- Mechanics: flat-pack up to 10 used bags inside one Sixty60 bag and leave it at
  the front door when the next delivery is due.
- Trial ran in late January 2021; national roll-out from early March 2021.
- Positioned as part of the Shoprite Group's zero-waste-to-landfill ambition.
- Sixty60 paper bags are FSC-certified.
- Group context figures quoted in the release: 37,312 tons of cardboard and
  4,410 tons of plastic recycled from distribution centres in the prior
  financial year (⚠️ single-source, company-stated).
- Scheme still promoted on Sixty60 social channels in 2023 ("give your old bags
  to your Sixty60 driver"); current 2026 status not confirmed ⚠️ UNVERIFIED.
