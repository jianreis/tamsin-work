## Summary

The best available quantification of how far South Africans actually live from a place that dispenses medicine, using National Income Dynamics Study panel data joined to Department of Health administrative facility coordinates.

The headline is deceptively reassuring: 90% of South Africans live within 7 km of the nearest public clinic and two-thirds live under 2 km away. The distribution matters more than the average. 14% of Black African adults live more than 5 km from the nearest facility against 4% of White adults, and 20% of households in the poorest income quintile are more than 5 km out against 5% in the richest. The residual is not random. It is poor, Black and rural, and it is the exact population a partner collection point would serve.

Two cautions before this gets used. First, these distances are to public clinics, not to retail pharmacies. Since the community pharmacy estate is far thinner than the clinic estate ([[src-access-ward-pharmacy-equity-2014]]), distance to a retail pharmacy is necessarily worse, but by how much is not quantified anywhere found. Second, the paper's own point is distance decay: utilisation falls with distance in a way that is steepest over the first few kilometres, so a 5 km threshold understates the behavioural effect. Related work using the 2009 and 2010 General Household Surveys (n=190,164) treats 30 minutes, roughly 2.5 km, as the availability threshold.

## Key claims

- 90% of South Africans live within 7 km of the nearest public clinic; two-thirds live less than 2 km away.
- 14% of Black African adults live more than 5 km from the nearest health facility, against 4% of White adults.
- 20% of households in the poorest income quintile are more than 5 km from the nearest health facility, against 5% of households in the top quintile.
- Approximately one third of Africans in rural areas are more than one hour's travel from the closest health care facility.
- Availability constraints (distance, transport cost, inconvenient opening times), concentrated in underdeveloped rural areas, were reported by 27% of respondents in the related survey analysis.
- The 30-minute mark, roughly 2.5 km, is used as the availability threshold in General Household Survey based analysis (2009 and 2010 GHS, n=190,164).
- Method: first nationally representative panel survey in South Africa (NIDS) combined with Department of Health administrative geographic data, testing the effect of distance to nearest facility on likelihood of a health consultation or an attended birth.
- All distances are to public health facilities, not to retail or community pharmacies.
