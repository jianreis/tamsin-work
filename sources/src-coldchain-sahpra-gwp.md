## Summary

SAHPRA's binding guideline for licensed wholesalers of medicines in South Africa,
version 5. It sets the temperature-control expectation for the storage and
movement of medicines: nobody in the chain may let a product sit outside its
registered storage conditions, and the controls that prove it (validation,
monitoring, records, calibrated equipment) must exist and be inspectable. The
guideline is explicitly aligned to WHO TRS 961 Annex 9 and the PIC/S Guide to
Good Distribution Practice, which is why the international GDP vocabulary applies
directly in South Africa.

Two limits matter for locker work. First, the guideline governs **wholesalers**,
not the pharmacy-to-patient hand-off, which sits under the Pharmacy Act and SAPC
Good Pharmacy Practice. Second, it does not contemplate unattended storage: the
model in the document is a licensed site with a responsible pharmacist and a
validated vehicle. An unattended locker at a distance from a pharmacy is not
addressed either way.

## Key claims

- All parties in the supply chain are responsible for handling, storage and
  distribution in a way that does not risk exposure to temperatures outside the
  product's recommended storage conditions.
- The stated reason is quality: excursions from recommended storage conditions
  can affect quality, safety and effectiveness of the product.
- Cold-chain processes are specifically addressed, and wholesalers must hold a
  Validation Master Plan covering the company's approach to validation.
- Temperature monitoring must occur at defined intervals; records must be
  available for review; monitoring equipment must be checked at predetermined
  intervals with the check results recorded and retained.
- The guideline aligns to **WHO Technical Report Series No. 961 (2011) Annex 9**,
  "Model guidance for the storage and transport of time- and temperature-sensitive
  pharmaceutical products", and to the **PIC/S Guide to Good Distribution Practice
  for Medicinal Products (June 2014)**.
- Scope is wholesaling. The guideline does not set rules for an unattended
  collection point or a locker sited away from a licensed pharmacy.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "All parties involved in the supply chain of pharmaceutical products are
> responsible for the effective, efficient and safe handling, storage and
> distribution of such products in a manner that does not risk exposure to
> temperatures outside of their recommended storage conditions."

> equipment used for monitoring should be checked at suitable predetermined
> intervals and the results of such checks should be recorded and retained."
