## Summary

The traffic between banks and retailers in South Africa already runs in one
direction, and it is the opposite of the one the concept proposes. Capitec puts
its services into retail stores rather than pulling retail services into its
branches. From 12 August 2025, Capitec clients could deposit cash at Pick n Pay
tills across about 1,400 stores, on top of withdrawals at Pick n Pay, Shoprite,
Checkers and Boxer tills for a R2.00 fee. This is worth reading two ways. It
proves that a bank and a retailer can run a counter service on each other's
premises with settled economics and staff trained to do it. It also shows
Capitec's revealed preference: when it wants reach, it rents someone else's
till rather than filling its own branch.

## Key claims

- From **12 August 2025**, Pick n Pay extended its Capitec partnership to include
  **cash deposits at Pick n Pay tills**, across roughly **1,400 store locations**.
- Capitec clients can **withdraw cash at Pick n Pay, Shoprite, Checkers and Boxer
  tills for a flat R2.00**, cheaper than the ATM tariff.
- Pick n Pay describes its in-store banking network as serving customers from
  **more than 11 financial institutions**.
- The big four South African banks **removed 8,345 ATMs over five years**, citing
  maintenance cost and security, and turned to retail partnerships for reach.
- Capitec's own materials describe **more than 7,000 ATMs at branches and
  retailers**, meaning a large share of its ATM estate already sits on
  third-party premises.
