# SAPC's own framing of control over scheduled medicine

## Summary

A stakeholder e-Note in which Council states how it thinks about control over
scheduled medicines. The framing is duty-based rather than location-based: the
pharmacist, and any other registered person assisting in dispensing within their
scope, carries a **professional responsibility to exercise proper and reasonable
care in respect of and control over medicines**, under the Rules relating to the
Code of Conduct made under s35A(b)(i) of the Pharmacy Act. Council also flags
rule 1.9.5 on medicine misuse and the risk of excessive quantities of
misuse-liable medicines.

That framing cuts both ways for the locker. A duty of care and control does not
demand physical presence, so remote control of a locker is arguable. But it does
mean the pharmacy cannot discharge its duty by pointing at the bank's security
arrangements. Whoever holds the parcel, the pharmacist still owns the outcome.

Council pairs this with a restatement of the s22A prohibition: no person other
than a pharmacist, pharmacist intern or pharmacist's assistant under a
pharmacist's personal supervision may sell a Schedule 1 to Schedule 6 substance
for the listed purposes without a Director-General permit.

## Key claims

- The pharmacist, and any other registered person assisting in the dispensing
  process within their registered scope, has a professional responsibility to
  **exercise proper and reasonable care in respect of and control over
  medicines**, per the Rules relating to the Code of Conduct (s35A(b)(i)).
- Council draws attention to **rule 1.9.5 (medicine misuse)** and to excessive
  quantities of medicines liable to be misused.
- Restatement of the statutory prohibition: no person other than a pharmacist,
  pharmacist intern or pharmacist's assistant acting under the **personal
  supervision** of a pharmacist may sell or export a Schedule 1 to Schedule 6
  substance for the listed purposes without a Director-General permit.
- The duty is framed as care and control over the medicine, not as presence at a
  place. No text retrieved locates the duty geographically.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "A pharmacist, and any other registered person who may assist in the dispensing
> process in line with the scope of practice for which they are registered, has
> the professional responsibility to exercise proper and/or reasonable care in
> respect of and control over medicines, in accordance with the Rules relating to
> the Code of Conduct for pharmacists and other persons registered in terms of the
> Pharmacy Act published in terms of Section 35A(b)(i) of the Pharmacy Act, 53 of
> 1974 as amended."

> "No person, other than a pharmacist, pharmacist intern or pharmacist's assistant
> acting under the personal supervision of a pharmacist, shall sell or export a
> Schedule 1, Schedule 2, Schedule 3, Schedule 4, Schedule 5 or Schedule 6
> substance for analytical purposes, manufacture of foods, cosmetics, educational
> or scientific purposes, unless a permit has been obtained from the
> Director-General for such purpose."
