## Summary

Peer-reviewed review of why manufacturer blister packs are hard to recycle:
they are thin laminates of plastic (PVC, often with PVDC or other barrier
layers, or cold-form aluminium) bonded to aluminium lidding foil, so the layers
must be separated before either material can be recovered. The paper surveys
mechanical, thermal and solvent separation routes. It is the technical basis for
treating blister waste as a specialist stream rather than kerbside recycling.
Direct fetch blocked on 2026-09-07; claims from search extracts of the
ScienceDirect and institutional-repository copies.

## Key claims

- Pharmaceutical blisters generate significant solid waste and typically
  contain plastics and aluminium as thin bonded layers.
- The multi-layer structure makes them hard to recycle; plastic and aluminium
  must be separated before recycling.
- The review covers characterisation of blister types (including PVC/PVDC and
  aluminium cold-form) and separation techniques for the layers.
- A related study (Waste Management, 2020) examines green solvents to recover
  aluminium and plastic from blister waste.
