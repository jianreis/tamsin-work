# NON-SA. Britain needed new legislation to let a non-pharmacist hand over a bagged script

## Summary

The most instructive comparator available, because the United Kingdom asked
precisely the question this vault is asking and answered it by changing the law.
Until January 2026, a checked and bagged prescription could not lawfully be
handed to a patient in the pharmacist's absence. From **7 January 2026** a
pharmacist may **authorise any competent member of the pharmacy team to hand out
checked and bagged prescriptions while the pharmacist is absent** or treated as
absent. A second tranche, from **10 December 2026**, lets pharmacists authorise a
pharmacy technician to undertake or supervise preparation, assembly, dispensing
and the sale and supply of medicines.

Three things transfer to the South African question. First, the handover of an
already-checked parcel was treated as a **supervised act** requiring the
pharmacist, not as mere delivery, which is the restrictive reading of the same
ambiguity that sits in Medicines Act s22A(5). Second, resolving it took a
statutory instrument, not a regulator's guidance note. Third, even after reform
the person handing over is a **competent member of the pharmacy team**, working
under a pharmacist's authorisation. Britain liberalised toward a trained human,
not toward an unattended machine at a third party's premises.

Marked NON-SA. This is persuasive context for a SAPC engagement, not authority in
South African law.

## Key claims

- From **7 January 2026**, pharmacists may authorise, when absent or treated as
  absent, the **handing out of checked and bagged prescriptions**.
- "Checked and bagged prescriptions" are defined in the commentary as **POM or P
  medicines checked for clinical appropriateness and accuracy, dispensed by or
  under the supervision of a pharmacist, and ready for sale or supply** with no
  further pharmacist intervention needed.
- The person handing them out must be a **competent member of the pharmacy
  team**, authorised by the pharmacist.
- A second tranche from **10 December 2026** allows a pharmacist to authorise a
  **pharmacy technician** to undertake or supervise preparation, assembly,
  dispensing, and the sale and supply of medicines.
- The change required **primary-legislation-level reform** (a statutory
  instrument approved by Parliament and the Privy Council), which is direct
  evidence that under the prior law the handover of a bagged script was not
  lawful without the pharmacist.
- The stated policy driver was mundane: allowing collection while the supervising
  pharmacist is on a lunch break.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "From 7 January 2026, pharmacists will be able to authorise - when absent or
> treated as absent - the handing out of checked and bagged prescriptions."

> "Pharmacists will be able to authorise any competent member of the pharmacy team
> to hand out prescriptions in their absence that have been checked and are ready
> to go to the patient without any further intervention from the pharmacist.
> These are referred to as 'checked and bagged prescriptions' which are
> prescription only (POM) or pharmacy (P) medicines which have been checked for
> clinical appropriateness and accuracy and dispensed by or under the supervision
> of a pharmacist and are ready for sale or supply."

> "[Proposals included] pharmacy team members handing out (supplying) bagged and
> dispensed medicines to patients at pharmacy premises in the absence of a
> pharmacist, for example to allow collection when the supervising pharmacist is
> on a lunch break."
