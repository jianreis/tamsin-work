# GPP rule 2.7 - dispensing has three phases, and the third is counselling

## Summary

GPP splits dispensing into three phases: interpret and evaluate the
prescription, prepare and label the medicine, then provide information and
instructions to the patient so the medicine is used safely and effectively. The
third phase is where the locker concept has to do its work, because a machine
cannot perform it. The rule states the obligation without stating a channel: it
requires that information and instructions be provided, and nothing retrieved in
this session requires that provision to be face to face, synchronous, or
co-located with the handover of the parcel. That silence is the whole argument
for remote counselling, and it is also the reason the answer is not certain.
GPP was written for a counter.

Phase three is also what separates a locker from a courier. Courier delivery has
an explicit GPP standard that substitutes a patient information leaflet for the
counter conversation (see
[[src-reg-gpp-courier-mail-delivery-standard]]). No equivalent standard has been
found for a static collection point.

## Key claims

- The dispensing process under GPP has **three phases**: (1) interpretation and
  evaluation of the prescription; (2) preparation and labelling of the prescribed
  medicine; (3) provision of information and instructions to the patient to
  ensure the safe and effective use of the medicine.
- **Rule 2.7** carries the minimum standards for dispensing medicine or scheduled
  substances; phase 3 is expressed as ensuring **optimum use** of the medicine.
- The rule does **not**, in any text retrieved, prescribe the medium of the phase
  3 interaction. No requirement of physical presence, no requirement that it
  happen at the moment of handover, no prohibition on telephone or messaging.
- GPP applies to every registered pharmacist, every pharmacy support person and
  every licensed pharmacy owner, and compliance is enforced through SAPC
  inspection. A pharmacy cannot contract out of phase 3 by outsourcing the
  handover.
- GPP's obligatory force runs through **s35A(b)(ii) of the Pharmacy Act** plus
  regulation 20(1) (Practice of Pharmacy), regulation 7(a) (Ownership and
  Licensing) and regulation 18(7)(b) of the Medicines Act General Regulations.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "The dispensing process consists of … interpretation and evaluation of the
> prescription; the preparation and labelling of the prescribed medicine; and the
> provision of information and instructions to the patient."

> "Provision of information and instructions to the patient to ensure the optimum
> use of medicine." (phase 3, rule 2.7)

> "Rule 2.7 of the GPP contains the Minimum Standards for dispensing medicine or
> scheduled substances."

> "Pharmacists and registered pharmacy owners issued with a licence in terms of
> section 22 of the Pharmacy Act must adhere to the standards and rules set out in
> the Council's Rules as to Good Pharmacy Practice in South Africa, made in terms
> of section 35A(b)(ii) of the Pharmacy Act (Act 53 of 1974)."
