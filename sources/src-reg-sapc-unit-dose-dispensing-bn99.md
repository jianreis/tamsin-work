# How SAPC actually sanctions a new piece of dispensing automation

## Summary

Useful less for what it decides than for how it decides. Pharmacists asked
Council for guidance on the safe use of unit dose dispensers; Council developed a
minimum standard, published it for comment as Board Notice 99 on 31 August 2018
under s35A(b)(ii), and added it to the GPP rules as an additional standard. That
is the working precedent for getting a new dispensing technology recognised in
South Africa: a request from the profession, a drafted minimum standard, a board
notice for comment, then absorption into GPP.

Read against the locker question, it says the route to a lawful
dispense-then-locker model probably runs through a new GPP minimum standard
rather than through an amendment to the Pharmacy Act or the Medicines Act. It
also says the route is slow and consultative, and that Council prefers to answer
technology questions by writing a standard rather than by ruling on individual
schemes.

## Key claims

- SAPC received requests from pharmacists for guidance on the **safe use of unit
  dose dispensers** during the dispensing of medicines to patients.
- Council developed a **minimum standard for unit dose dispensing (UDD)**,
  published for comment on **31 August 2018 as Board Notice 99**, as an
  **additional standard within the Rules relating to Good Pharmacy Practice**,
  made under **s35A(b)(ii)** of the Pharmacy Act.
- A further amendment, **BN 172 of 2019**, carried unit dose dispensing into the
  GPP rules (see [[src-disp-sapc-gpp-manual]]).
- The mechanism demonstrated: profession asks, Council drafts a minimum standard,
  board notice for comment, standard folded into GPP. No primary legislation was
  needed to accommodate the technology.
