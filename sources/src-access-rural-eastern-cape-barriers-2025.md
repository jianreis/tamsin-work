## Summary

A recent qualitative study of what it actually costs a rural chronic patient to fetch medicine. Fieldwork ran November to December 2024 at selected rural Eastern Cape facilities with 32 participants, 23 patients and 9 health professionals.

The number worth carrying: the median time to travel to the nearest clinic is 81 minutes, and the usual mode is minibus taxi. Patients described defaulting on medication when they could not raise taxi fare, when rain made the trip impossible, or when they could not give up a whole day. That is the mechanism connecting geographic distance to non-adherence, stated by patients rather than inferred.

The sample is small and province-specific, so it evidences the mechanism and supplies patient voice, not national prevalence. Pair it with [[src-access-mclaren-distance-decay-2014]] for the distribution and with [[src-access-last-kilometre-delivery-2021]] for stated demand for a delivery or collection alternative.

The paper also records MSF and the Eastern Cape Department of Health setting up supported pick-up points in hard-to-reach rural settings for diabetes medicine collection. That is a working precedent for the partner-collection-point pattern, already operating in the public sector.

## Key claims

- Study design: qualitative, 32 participants (23 patients, 9 health professionals), selected rural Eastern Cape facilities, data collected November to December 2024.
- Median travel time to the nearest clinic reported as 81 minutes; the common transport mode is minibus taxi.
- Patients default on medication when they cannot afford taxi fare, when weather prevents travel, or when they cannot be away from home for a full day.
- Transport expenditure causes financial distress for patients already living in poverty.
- Three themes: barriers to access, experiences and perspectives, existing strategies or interventions.
- Systemic drivers named: workforce shortages, infrastructure deficits, socioeconomic conditions, uneven resource distribution.
- MSF, with the Eastern Cape Department of Health, has established supported pick-up points in hard-to-reach rural settings for diabetes medicine collection.
