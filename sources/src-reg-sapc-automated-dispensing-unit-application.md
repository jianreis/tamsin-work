# SAPC's Automated Dispensing Unit conditions: the machine serves the pharmacist, not the patient

## Summary

SAPC runs a formal application route for an Automated Dispensing Unit, with
published requirements and conditions, submitted to the Registrar with the
Responsible Pharmacist's particulars and the pharmacy's annual registration
certificate attached. Two conditions in that form decide how Council currently
thinks about pharmacy automation, and both point away from a patient-facing
locker. The unit **delivers scheduled medicines to the pharmacist**, and
**access is limited to an authorised pharmacist, pharmacist intern or
pharmacist's assistant**. The machine is an instrument inside the professional
loop, and the patient is not among the parties permitted to open it.

That is the single most directly adverse finding for a bank-branch locker
released on a PIN to a patient. It is not a prohibition of lockers, because an
ADU as SAPC defines it is a different object from a locker holding parcels that
were already dispensed and checked elsewhere. But it establishes Council's
starting posture on automated units: restricted access, pharmacist-facing,
attached to a registered pharmacy with a named RP.

The form also shows the mechanism by which any new automation gets sanctioned.
It is an application to the Registrar against published conditions, not a
statutory amendment, which means the locker question plausibly has an
administrative answer rather than a legislative one.

## Key claims

- SAPC operates a dedicated **application for an Automated Dispensing Unit**,
  made in terms of the Pharmacy Act, 1974, submitted to the Registrar.
- The applicant must **acknowledge the requirements and conditions relating to an
  ADU as published by Council**.
- Among those conditions: the **ADU delivers scheduled medicines to the
  pharmacist**, and **access is limited to only an authorised pharmacist,
  pharmacist intern and pharmacist's assistant**.
- The application requires the **personal particulars of the responsible
  pharmacist** and a copy of the **annual Pharmacy Registration Certificate**, so
  an ADU is treated as an appendage of a registered pharmacy, not a standalone
  site.
- Incomplete applications lapse: Council considers the application null and void
  after 60 days if the necessary documents are not submitted.
- Nothing in the retrieved content addresses an ADU sited **away from the
  pharmacy premises**, nor patient-operated access.

## Verbatim excerpts

> pharmacist assistant."
