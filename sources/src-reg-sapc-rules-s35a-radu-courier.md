## Summary

The section 35A rules, read directly from the primary PDF supplied by Tamsin on
2026-08-16. Two rules here settle questions the rest of the research could only
circle.

**Rule 1.11 is the concept, already written.** SAPC has a published minimum
standard for a **Remote Automated Dispensing Unit** - an automated unit "remotely
operated from a pharmacy" that dispenses **chronic medicine** to a patient who is
nowhere near a pharmacist, with phases 1 and 3 of dispensing performed by a
pharmacist **through an audio-visual interface at the time of dispensing**.
Council's own stated rationale is improving access and relieving the burden on
facilities. Every design feature the earlier research recommended - fail-closed
pharmacist availability, a live link, chronic-only scope, guaranteed connectivity
- is already mandated text.

**The catch is one word in the heading: institutional public.** The standard is
written for public-sector pharmacy. The ask of Council is therefore not to invent
a category but to **extend an existing standard to community pharmacy** - a far
narrower and better-precedented request.

**Rule 1.9.7 restricts courier delivery in a way that bites the acute journey.**
Medicines prescribed for acute conditions **shall not be delivered by
mail/courier**, and medicines stored below 8°C may not be unless cold chain is
ensured.

## Key claims

- **RADU is defined** as an automated dispensing unit remotely operated from a
  pharmacy to dispense medicines and medical devices for long-term (chronic)
  therapy.
- **Council's stated purpose is access**: innovation and technology to improve
  access to pharmaceutical services, given pharmacy workforce shortage and disease
  burden, "to alleviate the patient burden on public facilities".
- **Scope is public sector**: the heading reads "minimum standards for
  institutional public pharmacy operating a remote automated dispensing unit", and
  1.11.1 says pharmacies **in the public sector** may use a RADU.
- **Chronic only** - 1.11.4(b) limits a RADU to medicines and devices for long-term
  therapy.
- **A pharmacist performs phases 1 and 3 through an audio-visual interface at the
  time of dispensing** - 1.11.5(a). Phase 3 is the counselling and supply step, so
  the patient is served by a live pharmacist over video, not by a machine alone.
- One prescription at a time; every RADU prescription **signed by the pharmacist**.
- **Refused medicine is locked in a secure cabinet** and may not be dispensed again
  until checked - 1.11.5(e).
- **A pharmacist must be accessible** to respond to all enquiries from a patient
  using the RADU - 1.11.5(f).
- **Where the RADU is not operating 24 hours, a designated pharmacist must be
  available for emergency supply** - 1.11.5(g). Contact details of the
  pharmacist-on-duty and the operational hours must be **displayed visibly at the
  unit** - 1.11.5(h)-(i).
- **Connectivity must be certified**: the responsible pharmacist must give Council
  an assessment certificate from the internet service provider proving adequate
  connectivity for patient-to-pharmacist communication - 1.11.6(c).
- Labels must carry **the address of the RADU** as well as the operating pharmacy,
  so the dispensing unit is identifiable - 1.11.5(d).
- Only registered pharmacy personnel may access the RADU for stock management and
  dispensing - 1.11.4(f).
- **Rule 1.9.7 (indirect supply by mail/courier)**: preference is for the
  pharmacist to give medicine directly; where mail or courier is necessary, the
  pharmacist must ensure protective packaging through the delivery process, a
  control system to detect and correct delay, a report-back system, and a patient
  information leaflet where available. Supply "to a person who receives medicine on
  behalf of the patient" is contemplated.
- **Rule 1.9.7(e): acute medicines may not be couriered.** Medicines prescribed for
  acute ailments or conditions - immediate need, not repeatable, non-chronic -
  shall not be delivered to patients by mail or courier.
- **Rule 1.9.7(f)**: medicines registered for storage below 8°C shall not be
  delivered by mail or courier unless cold chain management is ensured.

## Verbatim excerpts

> 1.11 MINIMUM STANDARDS FOR INSTITUTIONAL PUBLIC PHARMACY OPERATING A REMOTE AUTOMATED DISPENSING UNIT (RADU)

> Pharmacies in the public sector may use the Remote Automated Dispensing Unit (RADU) for purposes of dispensing medicine and devices for long term therapy (Chronic) in order to improve access to medicines and to alleviate the patient burden on public facilities.

> Remote Automated Dispensing Unit (RADU) - refers to an automated dispensing unit that is remotely operated from a pharmacy to dispense medicines and medical devices for long term therapy (chronic medicine).

> 1.11.5(a) Phase 1 and 3 of the dispensing processes must be performed only by a pharmacist through the audio-visual interface, at the time of dispensing.

> 1.11.5(e) If a patient refuses the dispensed medicine by a RADU, the medicine must be locked in a secure cabinet in the RADU and may not be dispensed until checked by a pharmacist or pharmacy support personnel for quality assurance purposes.

> 1.11.5(f) A pharmacist must be accessible to respond to all enquiries and or requests from a patient when utilising the RADU.

> 1.11.5(g) If the RADU is not operational on a 24-hour basis, a designated pharmacist(s) must be available for the emergency supply of pharmaceutical services.

> 1.11.5(h) Contact details of the pharmacist-on-duty must be displayed visibly at the RADU.

> 1.11.6(c) provide proof to Council, in the form of an assessment certificate from the internet service provider (ISP), that there is adequate connectivity to facilitate communication between the patients and the pharmacist operating the RADU;

> 1.9.7 Although it is preferable for a pharmacist to give medicines directly to the patient /caregiver, when it is necessary to sell, supply or deliver medicine or scheduled substances by or through the mail/courier to a patient or to members of the public or to a person who receives medicine on behalf of the patient, the pharmacist must ensure that—

> 1.9.7(e) medicines which are prescribed for acute ailments or conditions (i.e. immediate need, not repeatable and non-chronic conditions) shall not be delivered to patients by mail/courier;

> 1.9.7(f) medicines that are registered to be stored in conditions under 8ºC shall not be delivered to patients by mail/courier unless cold chain management is ensured.
