## Summary

The industry's own assessment of what load-shedding did to South African cold
chain, based on input from 15 GCCA members and energy experts. The headline damage
is not only spoiled stock. Repeated power cycling strains refrigeration plant, so
the cost shows up as maintenance and equipment failure as well as loss, and it
accumulates even in periods when the grid holds.

The recommendations are revealing about what mitigation actually costs: the report
asks government to suspend import duties and VAT on batteries and solar components
for a year, which is a signal that self-generation and storage, not grid recovery,
is what operators were relying on. Any cold-chain locker sited away from a
pharmacy inherits that same bill, per site.

The report is about the food and perishables cold chain more than pharmaceuticals,
so read the mechanism across rather than the numbers.

## Key claims

- Commissioned by the GCCA Africa Advisory Committee, conducted by **Tutwa
  Consulting Group**, published October 2024.
- Draws on qualitative and quantitative input from **15 GCCA members** plus energy
  experts, business leaders and researchers.
- Cold-chain resilience in South Africa is threatened by load-shedding and
  overreliance on ageing coal-fired plant.
- **Frequent power outages during load-shedding strain refrigeration equipment,
  leading to increased maintenance and breakdowns**, over and above product loss.
- Recommends suspending import duties and VAT on batteries and solar components for
  one year, to make backup power affordable.
- Scope is primarily the food and perishables cold chain, not pharmaceuticals.
