## Summary

The number that sizes the exclusion. IQVIA's analysis found that in 2021 cold-chain
pharmaceuticals were **0.2% of prescription volume** and **32% of industry
revenue**. Almost every script is shelf-stable; almost a third of the money is not.

That asymmetry is the whole design tension. Excluding cold chain from a locker rail
costs almost nothing in volume terms and removes the hardest engineering problem.
It also excludes the therapy classes that are growing fastest: 43% of the 292
specialty drugs approved between January 2018 and March 2023 required cold-chain
storage, and 6% required freezing. A rule that is cheap today gets more expensive
each year as GLP-1s and biologics move into ordinary chronic care.

These are United States figures. No equivalent South African split was found. The
SA cold-chain share is likely lower than 0.2% by volume in the public sector, where
CCMDD excludes insulin outright, and higher in private medical-scheme chronic
dispensing, where insulin and biologics are covered. `⚠️ UNVERIFIED` - inference,
not sourced.

## Key claims

- **Cold-chain pharmaceuticals were 0.2% of US prescription volume in 2021** while
  representing **32% of industry revenue** (IQVIA, "Pharma's Frozen Assets", 2023).
- Corroborating restatement: temperature-controlled medications are about 0.2% of
  prescription volume yet drive nearly one-third of pharmaceutical value.
- **43% of 292 drugs approved between January 2018 and March 2023 required
  cold-chain storage; 6% required freezing.** The direction of travel is towards
  more cold chain, not less.
- Growth is concentrated in biologics and GLP-1 medicines.
