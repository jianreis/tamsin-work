## Summary

Peer-reviewed evidence that a meaningful share of low-income, **uninsured**
South Africans pay cash to see **private GPs** for primary care, and what that
costs them. Grounds the "local doctor" script origin for the cash acute
journey: a paid consult ending in a paper script is a private-GP story, not a
public-clinic one (public PHC is free and dispenses on site). The companion
SAMJ 2019 paper maps the private low-cost PHC delivery models serving this
segment.

## Key claims

- Low-income, uninsured patients use **private GPs as a first contact** for
  acute primary care in meaningful numbers, alongside free public clinics.
- **Mean cash fee for an uninsured adult visiting a private practitioner:
  ~R311; a common fee point was R300** (fieldwork period pricing; note the
  date when quoting).
- The cash fee covered varying bundles: consultation, sometimes diagnostics,
  and **medicines where the GP held a dispensing licence** - i.e. many of
  these local doctors hand over the medicine themselves (the §22C dispensing-
  doctor regime, [[src-reg-medicines-act-101-1965]]).
- Reasons for choosing a paid private visit over a free clinic: shorter
  waits, convenient hours, perceived quality and dignity - the same trip-and-
  queue economics the vault documents elsewhere
  [[wiki/consumer-context/two-economy-affordability]].
