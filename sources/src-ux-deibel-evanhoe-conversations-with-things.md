# Summary

The standard practitioner text on designing chat and voice interfaces. Its
relevance to the #11 work is its treatment of how real dialogue works and how
to write bot turns: turn-taking as the basic unit, repair as a first-class
design object (people constantly mend misunderstandings mid-conversation, and
systems must too), and prompt-writing as a craft of clear, concise,
contextual lines. It also foregrounds inclusion and cultural nuance in
"talking tech", which matters for a multi-language SA channel.

# Key claims

- The chapter "Talking Like a Person" treats **turn-taking, repair, and
  cultural nuance** as the mechanics that make a system's talk feel natural.
- **Repair** is a normal part of human conversation, so a conversational
  system needs designed repair moves, not just error messages.
- "Designing Prompts" treats bot lines as writing craft: **clear, concise,
  contextual** - each prompt written for its exact place in the flow.
- The book's personality framework ties trust to consistency: the system
  should sound like one person with one register throughout.
- Ethics and inclusion (whose language, whose norms) are design inputs for
  conversational systems, not afterthoughts.
