## Summary

The consolidated GPP manual, read **directly from the primary PDF** supplied by
Tamsin on 2026-08-16 and archived at `sources/pdfs/`. This supersedes the
search-extracted readings in the earlier `src-reg-*` notes, several of which were
wrong.

Four rules govern whether medicine may be handed to a patient away from a
pharmacy counter. Two of them are more restrictive than the earlier research
suggested, and two open a route that the research missed entirely.

**The prohibition.** Rule 1.9.3(b) expressly forbids a patient from receiving
directly from an automated dispensing unit. This is not a gap or a silence - it
is a stated rule, and it is the single sentence that blocks an unattended
customer-facing collection unit today.

**The opening.** Rule 1.10 already permits a pharmacy to serve a patient
**through an opening in its exterior wall** without the patient entering, so long
as a pharmacist is in two-way visual and auditory contact and does the
counselling. South Africa has had a lawful pattern for wall-side service since
2006; what it has never had is a version with nobody on the other side.

Both rules arrived by board notice - 1.10 by BN 12 of 10 March 2006, 1.9 by BN 50
of 27 February 2015 - confirming that Council extends GPP administratively as
dispensing technology changes.

## Key claims

- **Rule 1.9.3(b): a patient may not receive from an ADU.** Verbatim below. Applies
  to the patient, their caregiver and their agent.
- **Rule 1.9's scope is not institutional-only.** Its stated purpose covers
  community pharmacies, institutional pharmacies, dispensaries in primary health
  care clinics and medicine rooms of licensed dispensers. The earlier claim that
  the automation standard is written for public institutional pharmacy is wrong.
- **Rule 1.9.4(a): phases 1 and 3 of dispensing must be done by a pharmacist,**
  pharmacy support personnel or licensed dispenser. Phase 3 is the provision of
  information to the patient, so the counselling and handover step cannot be
  automated away.
- **Rule 1.9.4(c) already requires reachability:** the pharmacist must be
  accessible to respond to enquiries about anything dispensed from an ADU.
- **Rule 1.9.4(d): every prescription dispensed from an ADU must be signed in
  person** by the pharmacist or licensed dispenser.
- **Rule 1.10 permits exterior-wall service.** A drive-through facility is defined
  as elements "located within and without the dispensary department" enabling
  service to a patient who does not leave their vehicle, and the term expressly
  includes a window or other opening in the exterior wall. A pharmacy may not
  operate one **without** such an opening.
- **Rule 1.10.2 sets the conditions:** two porticos, one to receive the
  prescription and one to supply the medicine; two-way visual and auditory
  communication; counselling delivered without losing that contact; and door
  access so the pharmacist can hand medicine directly where the patient is not in
  the driver's seat.
- **Rule 2.7.1.3.1(a): physical presence is "preferred", not required.** Advising
  the patient or their agent must be carried out by a pharmacist or other
  authorised person, with physical presence preferred - which leaves room for
  remote counselling.
- **Rule 2.7.5(d): delivered medicine must be received by an adult over 14, or
  returned to the pharmacy.** A locker is not an adult. This bears on every
  unattended-collection design, not only automated ones.
- **Rule 2.7.5(b):** where a non-pharmacist delivers, the pharmacist must furnish
  comprehensive written instructions including patient details, correct use, the
  prescribed statement and a patient information leaflet.
- **Rule 2.7.5(a):** all efforts must be made to enable face-to-face counselling
  by a pharmacist.

## Verbatim excerpts

Read directly from the PDF; page references are to the consolidated manual.

> 1.9.3(b) The installation and the location of the ADU must be such that medicines and medical devices are not accessed and/or received directly by the patient or his or her caregiver or agent.

> 1.9.4(a) Phase 1 and Phase 3 of dispensing must be done by the pharmacist, pharmacy support personnel or licensed dispenser, within their scope of practice.

> 1.9.4(c) The pharmacist or licensed dispenser must be accessible to respond to inquiries or requests pertaining to all medicines and medical devices dispensed from an ADU.

> 1.9.4(d) All prescription dispensed from ADU must be signed in person by the pharmacist or licensed dispenser.

> 1.9.2 The purpose of this standard is to regulate the use of ADUs in community pharmacies, institutional pharmacies, dispensaries in primary health care clinics and medicine rooms of licensed dispensers.

> 1.10 'Drive-through' facility means any combination of structural, mechanical, electronic or other elements located within and without the dispensary department of a licensed pharmacy. This facility enables the personnel of the pharmacy to provide pharmaceutical services to a patient who drives a vehicle to the pharmacy, without the personnel of the pharmacy leaving the dispensary department or the patient leaving his/her vehicle. The term includes, a window or other opening in the exterior wall of a prescription department of a licensed pharmacy.

> 1.10.2(c) Provide two-way visual and auditory communication between the personnel of the pharmacy and the patient receiving pharmaceutical services;

> 1.10.2(e) Be equipped in order to allow the pharmacist, or intern pharmacist under the supervision of a pharmacist, to provide a patient, receiving pharmaceutical services with counselling without losing visual or auditory communication with the patient.

> A pharmacy shall not provide pharmaceutical services by, means of a drive-through facility, which does not include a window, or other opening, in the exterior wall of the pharmacy.

> 2.7.1.3.1(a) Advising a patient or the patient's agent/caregiver (physical presence is preferred) must be carried out by a pharmacist or other authorised person.

> 2.7.5(a) All efforts must be made to enable face-to-face counselling of the patient by a pharmacist.

> 2.7.5(d) In the absence of an adult (a person above 14 years) to receive the medicine, it must be taken back to the pharmacy.

> [Rule 1.9 added by BN 50 of 27 February 2015.]

> [Minimum standard added by BN 12 of 10 March 2006, renumbered for ease of reference.]
