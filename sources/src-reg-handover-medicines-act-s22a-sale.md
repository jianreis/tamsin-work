# Medicines Act s22A — who may sell a scheduled substance

## Summary

Section 22A(5) is the provision that decides the locker question, and it is
written in terms of **sale**, not of handover. A Schedule 2 to Schedule 6
substance may not be sold by anyone other than a pharmacist, a pharmacist intern
or a pharmacist's assistant acting under the personal supervision of a
pharmacist (plus the other authorised prescriber and s22C licence categories).
The statutory verb matters: if the sale is completed at the dispensing pharmacy
and the parcel is then transported to the patient, the later handover is a
delivery event that s22A(5) does not obviously regulate. If a regulator instead
reads the sale as concluded when the patient takes possession, an unattended
locker puts the sale outside the s22A(5) list of authorised sellers. Nothing
retrieved in this session settles which reading SAPC or SAHPRA would apply.

This note covers the handover angle only. General scheduling, repeat and pricing
provisions are already captured in [[src-reg-medicines-act-101-1965]].

## Key claims

- **s22A(5):** "Any Schedule 2, Schedule 3, Schedule 4, Schedule 5 or Schedule 6
  substance shall not be sold by any person other than …" followed by the
  authorised categories, of which the pharmacy category is a pharmacist, a
  pharmacist intern, or a pharmacist's assistant acting under the personal
  supervision of a pharmacist.
- **s22A(6)** sets the conditions on which a sale under (5) may take place
  (prescription requirements, repeats, records). It regulates the conditions of
  sale, not the physical place of handover.
- The Act's controls are drafted around **sale, possession, acquisition, supply
  and manufacture**. No provision found in this session uses the concepts of
  "collection point", "locker", "pick-up point" or "delivery to a third party".
- **s22A(15)** carries the Director-General permit machinery (exceptional access
  permits, Schedule 7 and 8 acquisition and possession). It is a permit route for
  specified substances and purposes, not a general power to license unattended
  handover of ordinary chronic medicine.
- A permit may be refused where the Director-General is of the opinion that the
  applicant cannot keep or store the substance so as to prevent loss or
  diversion. The same loss-and-diversion logic is what any locker proposal will
  be tested against, whatever the legal route.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "Any Schedule 2, Schedule 3, Schedule 4, Schedule 5 or Schedule 6 substance
> shall not be sold by any person other than …" (s22A(5))

> "No person, other than a pharmacist, pharmacist intern or pharmacist's
> assistant acting under the personal supervision of a pharmacist, shall sell or
> export a Schedule 1, Schedule 2, Schedule 3, Schedule 4, Schedule 5 or Schedule
> 6 substance for analytical purposes, manufacture of foods, cosmetics,
> educational or scientific purposes, unless a permit has been obtained from the
> Director-General for such purpose."

> "[A permit may not be issued if] the Director-General is of the opinion that
> the applicant is not capable of keeping or storing the substance in a manner so
> as to prevent the loss or diversion thereof."
