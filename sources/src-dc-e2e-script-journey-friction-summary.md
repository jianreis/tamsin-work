# Script Journey Blueprint — end-to-end friction summary

## Summary

Tamsin's own **synthesis of friction across the end-to-end pharmacy script
journey**, laid out as a nine-stage blueprint ("Script Journey Blueprint")
with two lanes per stage: **customer friction** (red) and **operational
inefficiencies** (yellow). It is the author's roll-up of the research
programme (walkthroughs, interviews, complaints, staff voice) rather than a
new data collection, so it carries claims at the theme level without
per-claim quotes. Transcribed in full below from the shared image; the raw
image file is not yet archived in `sources/pdfs/` — ⚠️ ask Tamsin for the
file or the source deck page if the artefact itself is needed.

**Reliability — B (author's synthesis of primary research, justify):** made

## The nine stages, transcribed

### 1. Script submitted
Script submitted by customer or healthcare provider via preferred channel
(in-store, WA, email, app, web, or integrated system).
- **Customer friction:** Inconsistent submission experience across channels ·
  No visibility on costs or stock before submission.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Manual in-store monitoring and response
  across WhatsApp, email, and call line · Intake bottlenecks during peak
  volumes · Non-dispensary queries creating interruptions · High multitasking
  load + fragmented systems = increased error risk.

### 2. Order received
Script received, confirmed, and processed through OCR/intake system.
- **Customer friction:** Inconsistent confirmation experience across
  channels · Exceptions not consistently communicated.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Scripts routed through multiple channels
  into store and central processing · Partial OCR capability · Unreliable
  connectivity · Profile lookup and verification delays.

### 3. Script captured & processed
Script captured and processed in pharmacy system, including medical aid
validation and cost determination.
- **Customer friction:** Inconsistent communication on delays, stock issues,
  or costs · Customers must infer order-capture success based on cost
  received · No progress visibility.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Manual script capture; capture errors ·
  Fragmented systems requiring duplicate work · Slow systems slow work ·
  Manual resolution of unclear or poor-quality scripts — need to contact
  doctor or customer for clarification · Outdated or inaccurate stock file ·
  Manual regulatory record-keeping.

### 4. Order picked
Medication picked, labelled, and prepared for pharmacist verification.
- **Customer friction:** No progress visibility · Incorrect medication due to
  picking errors.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Most time-consuming part of process ·
  Non-responsive tablets · Picking errors.

### 5. Order checked
Pharmacist verifies and signs off order.
- **Customer friction:** No progress visibility.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Pharmacists balancing physical and virtual
  workloads · Device and system lag · Limited dedicated verification
  workspace · Inconsistent staging process.

### 6. Order packed
Medication packed into appropriate handover packaging with required labels
applied.
- **Customer friction:** Inconsistent packaging experience.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Duplicate or unnecessary labels · POPIA
  compliance risks · Manual label corrections · Inconsistent packing
  standards.

### 7. Order collated & dispatched (delivery)
Order collated and prepared for delivery.
- **Customer friction:** No progress visibility.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Fragmented and physically separated
  pharmacy-to-delivery handover · No standardised transfer process ·
  Dispensary storage inconsistencies.

### 8. Order collected
Medication handed over to customer with counselling.
- **Customer friction:** Confusing "ready" communication · Queue confusion ·
  Unacknowledged, longer-than-anticipated waits at counter · Slow profile
  lookup · Orders incorrect, incomplete and incorrect — customer waiting time
  extended while corrections are made · Unexpected co-payments.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Manual surname-based search · Rewrites and
  counter rework due to earlier processing errors · Inefficient and
  non-standardised order storage.

### 9. Order delivered
Order collected by delivery driver and delivered to customer.
- **Customer friction:** Unclear delivery timing · Lack of delivery
  communication, particularly if delayed due to stock issues · Confusion when
  parcels arrive separately · Incomplete or incorrect deliveries.
- **Operational inefficiencies:** Piccup process not consistently followed by
  in-store drivers · Limited visibility of delivery status back to pharmacy.

## Key claims

- The dominant customer-side pattern across stages 3–7 is a single theme:
  **no progress visibility** anywhere between submission and handover.
- Communication is the second spine: inconsistent submission/confirmation
  experiences (1–2), inconsistent delay/stock/cost communication (3),
  confusing "ready" communication (8), and lack of delivery communication (9).
- Money surprises book-end the journey: no cost visibility before submission
  (1), cost as the only capture signal (3), unexpected co-payments (8).
- Operationally, the through-line is **fragmented systems and manual work**:
  manual capture, duplicate work, manual label corrections, manual
  surname-based search, no standardised pharmacy-to-delivery transfer.
- Order picking is named the **most time-consuming part of the process** (4).
