## Summary

Insulin is the product that decides whether a locker rail can carry cold chain,
because it is the highest-volume refrigerated chronic medicine in South Africa and
the one CCMDD explicitly excludes. Its storage rules are more forgiving than
"2-8°C or nothing", and that nuance is where a design opportunity sits.

Unopened insulin belongs at 2-8°C. Once in use, most presentations tolerate room
temperature below 25-30°C for about 28 days, and the 28-day clock starts when the
vial or pen first reaches room temperature, not when it is first injected. Above
30°C, chemical stability is meaningfully compromised. Freezing destroys insulin
permanently, and thawing does not recover it.

The practical reading for a locker: a short, bounded, monitored ambient dwell is
not automatically fatal to insulin, but it consumes in-use shelf life the patient
has not been told about, and a South African forecourt in January routinely exceeds
30°C. Product-specific labelling governs; presentations differ.

## Key claims

- **Unopened insulin**: store refrigerated at **2-8°C** (36-46°F).
- **In-use insulin**: keep at room temperature **below 25-30°C** and use within a
  product-specific period, **typically 28 days**.
- The 28-day period **starts when the insulin first reaches room temperature**, not
  when the patient starts using it. Returning it to the fridge does not reset the
  clock.
- **Exposure above 30°C considerably compromises insulin's chemical stability.**
- **Freezing permanently damages insulin**; it must not be used even after thawing.
- Limits vary by product; manufacturer labelling for the specific presentation
  governs.

## Verbatim excerpts

> "Unopened insulin should be stored in the refrigerator between 36°F and 46°F

> "Once opened, insulin should be kept at room temperature below 77°F-86°F
> (25°C-30°C) and used within the recommended timeframe (typically 28 days)."

> "The 28-day period starts when insulin first reaches room temperature, not when
