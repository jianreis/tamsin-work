## Summary

Banks hosting parcel collection is a thin precedent. The strongest cases are
post offices rather than banks: InPost began installing lockers at UK Post
Office branches in November 2025, starting with two sites and a six-month trial
of several hundred, explicitly so customers can collect when the branch is
closed. On the banking side the examples are small and American: Amazon placed
Hub Lockers at ten Tinker Federal Credit Union sites in Oklahoma, and BBVA
Compass piloted package pickup at 11 of its 345 Texas branches. Nothing found
suggests either grew into a standing programme, and nothing found documents a
failure either, which is its own signal about how much attention these pilots
got. No South African precedent for parcel collection inside a bank branch was
found.

## Key claims

- **InPost installed its first lockers at UK Post Office branches in November
  2025**, starting at Stanley (Ilkeston, Derbyshire) and Lanesfield
  (Wolverhampton), as the opening of a **six-month trial covering several hundred
  sites**.
- The stated customer benefit is the ability to **send, receive and return
  parcels at the local Post Office even when it is closed** - the locker, not the
  counter, is what extends the hours.
- **Amazon Hub Lockers** have been placed at **ten Tinker Federal Credit Union
  locations** in metro Oklahoma City.
- **BBVA Compass**, a Texas bank with 345 branches, **piloted Amazon package
  pickup at 11 sites**.
- Amazon describes its pickup network as sited at "supermarkets, convenience
  stores, banks and other nearby locations", so banks are an acknowledged host
  category, but a minor one.
- **Poste Italiane's Punto Poste Locker** network exceeds 1,000 units in Italy,
  operated by an organisation that is simultaneously a bank (BancoPosta) and a
  logistics business. This is a combined postal-bank entity rather than a bank
  hosting an outside party's parcels.
- **No South African example was found** of a bank branch operating as a parcel
  collection point. The established South African collection networks -
  **Pargo (4,000+ points)** and **PUDO** - sit in retailers, forecourts and small
  businesses. Pargo's named hosts include Clicks, FreshStop at Caltex and Lewis;
  banks do not appear.
- **No documented failure case was found.** The absence of published post-mortems
  is not evidence the pilots succeeded.
