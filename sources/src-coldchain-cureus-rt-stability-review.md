## Summary

A 2025 review assembling published room-temperature stability data for refrigerated
medicines, motivated by exactly the situation a locker creates: unintended ambient
exposure during transport delays, power outages and handling errors. The authors
identified 150 refrigerated medications with usable room-temperature stability data.

Two findings are directly useful. First, an excursion is not automatically a
write-off, and a lot of stock is discarded that did not need to be, which is a real
cost line. Second, the evidence base is thin and brand-specific: two products with
the same active ingredient can have different excursion tolerances, and
manufacturers do not publish enough excursion data to support confident operational
rules. That second point is the one that should temper enthusiasm for
"monitored ambient dwell is fine for cold-chain items". Without brand-level
stability data in hand, an operator cannot tell a patient whether a given excursion
mattered.

## Key claims

- **150 refrigerated medications** were identified with available data supporting
  room-temperature stability.
- Unintended exposure of refrigerated medicines to room temperature occurs during
  **transport delays, power outages and handling errors**.
- **Evidence-based guidance on acceptable room-temperature excursions remains
  limited**, despite frequent real-world cold-chain breaches.
- **Brand-specific data must be prioritised**: stability profiles differ even among
  products with the same active ingredient.
- Broader manufacturer transparency on temperature-excursion studies is needed to
  support evidence-based decisions.
- There is a clinical and economic case for minimising unnecessary wastage after an
  excursion, rather than discarding by default.
