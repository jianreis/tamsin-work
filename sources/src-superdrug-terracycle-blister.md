## Summary

The UK's first pharmacy blister recycling scheme, and a lesson in what happens
when uptake outruns funding. TerraCycle's medicine-packet programme, sponsored
first by Sanofi, added all Superdrug pharmacies in January 2021, was swamped,
and by May 2021 dropped every participating independent pharmacy. Superdrug
became sponsor and the scheme now runs only in Superdrug pharmacy stores with
brand sponsors. Direct fetch blocked on 2026-09-07; claims from search extracts
of the Superdrug page, The Pharmaceutical Journal and TerraCycle UK help.

## Key claims

- TerraCycle medicine blister programme: packets collected, separated by
  polymer type, extruded into pellets for recycled products.
- All Superdrug pharmacies joined at the start of January 2021; TerraCycle
  reported "an encouragingly high uptake".
- Volumes exceeded the resources set up; infrastructure and funding were
  redesigned; in May 2021 all participating independent pharmacies were
  removed "owing to significant challenges to keep the collection viable".
- Superdrug replaced Sanofi as scheme sponsor.
- Today: collection boxes in Superdrug pharmacy stores only (not all stores),
  funded with brand partners (Benadryl, Buscopan, Calpol, Nurofen, Imodium,
  Nicorette, Optrex, Dulcolax, Sudafed, Allevia).
- Rules: empty blisters only; no bulk drop-offs; no bottles, aerosols or packs
  containing medicine.
- TerraCycle states all programme decisions (locations, quantities) sit with
  Superdrug.
