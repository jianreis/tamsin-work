## Summary

The "a bank branch is a secure place" argument holds up on the physical
evidence. SABRIC recorded two bank robberies in South Africa during 2025, down
from eight the year before, and ATM attacks fell 46% with associated cash losses
down 63%. Crime has moved to the digital channel, where reported losses hit
R2.4 billion across 110,074 investigations. The qualification is that the
industry got this result partly by taking cash out of branches and ATMs, so the
same trend that makes a branch physically safe also makes it a channel that
wants less cash and fewer over-the-counter transactions, not more.

## Key claims

- **Two bank robberies reported in 2025**, against eight in 2024. The two branch
  robberies yielded **R630,000**.
- **ATM attacks fell 46%** year on year; associated cash losses fell **63%**.
- Digital banking crime claims reached **R2.4 billion across 110,074
  investigations** in 2025, up **29.2%** on 2024 and more than double the
  R1.09 billion of 2023.
- **Banking-app crime accounted for more than 70%** of reported digital banking
  losses.
- **Card fraud losses rose 18% to R1.7 billion**, driven by card-not-present
  fraud.
- Earlier trend, for contrast: SABRIC reported an **85% rise in associated
  robbery incidents inside bank branches in 2023** (robberies of customers in or
  around the branch rather than of the bank itself). The 2025 picture supersedes
  it on bank robbery, but the customer-targeted risk category is separate and
  should not be assumed to have collapsed with it.
- Cash-in-transit robbery pressure is named as a reason banks have accelerated
  toward cashless operation, closing ATMs in high-risk areas and reducing
  cash-handling services in branches.
