# Seed prompt - #12: Thabo's acute script as a WhatsApp conversation

A self-contained brief for a fresh workspace. The job: give the **cash track
of the approved #10 acute journey** the same channel-mock treatment #11 gave
the #09 repeat journey - the literal WhatsApp threads on Thabo's phone, one
interactive phone frame per phase, each with an annotation rail naming the
WhatsApp Business Platform mechanic and the persona constraint it honours.
#11 is the pattern exemplar; this brief records what transfers, what is
different about acute, and the decisions that need Tamsin before or during
the build.

**2026-08-16 revision.** The #11 re-sync session (punchlist 15, closed) and a
parallel research/concepts session both landed after the first draft of this
brief. #11 was re-cut end to end against a sourced conversational-design

## Learnings from the 2026-08-16 #11 session - apply, don't rediscover

These were earned message by message with Tamsin on #11 and are now house
rules for any WhatsApp mock. The #12 script is written to them from the
first draft, not retrofitted.

1. **One turn, one job; one decision per message.** A message holding two
   offers and a question is three turns stuffed into one bubble - split it.
   Sequential small choices beat one big one. The #11 tell: the first sync
   draft put the offer, the constraint, the cost and a three-way choice in
   one message; the approved version is four turns, each with one job.
2. **The question sits last**, then the floor is yielded. Context above,
   one ask at the end, nothing after it.
3. **Buttons are the user's next words.** Max 3 and 20 characters (sourced),
   but 3 is a ceiling, not a norm - binary choices get 2. No emoji or icons
   in button labels or questions. Labels in his words ("How would it
   work?"), never system labels.
4. **Exploration language while exploring; commitment language only on the
   confirmation turn.** Never "Yes" or "Done" while he is still finding out
   what saying yes means. Acute's go-ahead gate is exactly where the one
   commitment button belongs.
5. **Read back any change to money, medicine or dates and get an explicit
   yes before acting.** In #12 this is the go-ahead itself, and any mid-day
   change after it.
6. **The visual ladder** (spec ground rule 3): rung 1, emoji as functional
   anchors (about one per line, marking what the line is; never decoration)
   plus native formatting - the price breakdown, status lines (✅ 📦) and
   dose lines (🌅 🍽 ✔) all use it; rung 2, **catalogue product cards**
   (thumbnail, name, price, add-to-order; 🛒 cart echoes back into the
   thread) wherever the choice is between physical products - in #12 that is
   the symptom add-on attach (10(c)) and any priced OTC options; rung 3,
   decorative media stays declined, and the one image that runs the OTHER
   way - Thabo's photo of the paper script - is his outgoing message, worth
   annotating as the deliberate exception.
7. **Real product names, owners never guessed.** Order, parcel list and any
   look-up use the same real names (illustrative ⚠️, availability and
   pricing unverified) so phone, pack and shelf match word for word. A
   dispensed medicine's owner is its patient - here the child - and the
   dose entry speaks to the carer. A till slip has no owner: OTC add-ons are
   (Household) unless someone says otherwise.
8. **Change is ever-present, never offered.** No inbound message body offers
   to change the plan; capability is furniture. In #11 that is the standing
   60-character template footer ("Reply CHANGE any time...") opening one
   9-row menu. #12 transfers the principle, not the artefact: propose an
   acute-appropriate footer (what can still change on a one-day order, and
   until when - e.g. before packing) and the menu behind it, sized to acute.
   Demonstrate it once as an optional branch that resets, like #11's C3/C4.
9. **User-pulled beats pushed - the answer-not-ambush test.** #11 re-hung
   its whole add-to-order phase off a button the customer taps, which also
   keeps everything inside the 24-hour service window (no marketing
   templates). #10's symptom add-on attach is service-initiated by design -
   keep #10's behaviour, but shape the message to pass the test: it rides
   the order he just placed, arrives once, reads as help with the diagnosis
   in front of them, and its products are cards he can ignore. If a draft
   still smells like an ambush, park it as a question for Tamsin rather than
   deciding alone.
10. **Internal reasoning stays in the rail.** #11's payday rule generalises:
    the service speaks only in facts the customer gave it, and the design's
    understanding of *why* he chose something (affordability, dignity) lives
    in the annotation rail, never in a message. For #12 the sharp edges are
    the money-isn't-there scenario (hold-at-counter, price lock - the chat
    states facts, never sympathy about his wallet) and the counselling
    opt-out (10(j)).
11. **Checks are explained in plain lines that answer "checked what?"** -
    the #11 C1 pattern: a registered pharmacist, one anchor line per check,
    and a concrete example doing the teaching. #12's version carries the
    paediatric weight check: the dose checked against the child's weight,
    so a parent collects with confidence (Tamsin's own wording - reuse it).
12. **Credentials are issued when the thing they open exists** (#11: PIN on
    locker arrival, nothing at sealing). #12's order code should follow the
    same physics - first issued when the order is actually ready at the
    collections counter, not as a promise earlier.
13. **Process that worked, verbatim:** propose each exchange as the literal
    thread in conversation and get Tamsin's wording notes before writing the
    spec; spec first, then the HTML mirrors it; commit per approved pass;
    every walkthrough driven headless in Chromium before commit (zero
    console errors) with phone screenshots sent for review; style greps on
    every .md before handoff.

## The agreed process (confirmed on #11 - repeat it)

- Feature mapping for all phases is **proposed in the build and reviewed in
  situ** - do not ask for mapping sign-off up front.
- **One phase is mocked copy-complete first**; the other phases render as
  mapped stubs until Tamsin approves the pass. Only then build the full set.
- Register is **annotated**: phone frame plus rail. Threads render in
  **English for the executive audience**, with the standing note that
  production renders them in the customer's chosen language.
- Copy fidelity: the mock invents **no new service behaviour**. Where #10
  says "the chat asks me how", #12 decides only what that message literally
  looks like.
- Silence is the golden path; buttons offer change, never demand
  confirmation - the go-ahead gate is acute's one sanctioned exception,
  alongside read-backs under learning 5.
- Rand amounts and product names are illustrative ⚠️. Platform capability
  claims cite the 2026-08-16 ingest; the commerce surface (catalogue, cart,
  product cards) still carries ⚠️ pending its own docs verify (punchlist 11).
- Walkthrough conventions: highlighted ▶ rows, cards and dashed "tap to
  send" pills mark the scripted path; optional branches play without
  rewriting the story; every phone has its own reset. Commit per pass,
  `vault:` prefix. #05-#11 are untouched.

## What is different about acute - design to these, don't copy #11

1. **The channel entry is a photo, not a subscription.** Thabo's journey
   starts with a paper script from the clinic, photographed INTO the thread -
   an outgoing media message from him, the one place the data cost runs his
   way. The 15-minute snap-to-price reply (punchlist 10(f)) is the answering
   beat, and ⭐ "I knew the price before I left the clinic gate" lands on it.
   (Snap-a-Script the concept is archived; the price moment now anchors in
   Real-Time Transparency - link accordingly.)
2. **Hours, not a monthly cycle.** One sick day: script sent from the clinic
   queue, price back before the gate, go-ahead, checked and filled, collected
   that afternoon - then the day-3 check-in, and P6 weeks later. Date pills
   and timestamps must carry that compression.
3. **The go-ahead reply is a gate, not a courtesy.** #10 rule: Thabo's
   "go ahead" is the legal and operational release - nothing is dispensed
   until it lands. This is the acute journey's one mid-flow confirmation,
   the counterpart of #11's sign-up read-back - and per learning 4, the one
   message that carries a commitment button.
4. **Collection is the collections counter, not a locker.** His store, named
   priority handover, ready-within-30, order code at handover (issued per
   learning 12), counselling worked into the handover, pay what he already
   knew at the counter. Express collect carries 🔮 requires-reform (10(j)) -
   the rail must say so honestly. No CCMDD, no Capitec.
5. **A child's medicine.** The paediatric weight check at the named final
   check, the dose in Thabo's language, and the unhurried pacing rule from
   the #10 passes: when the person is unwell, the copy never sells - which
   also tempers how the symptom add-on attach (learning 9) may read.
6. **No pushed reminders.** The day-3 check-in is the single touch, and his
   "no, he isn't better" answer escalates to a pharmacist phoning and a
   booked clinic visit with the price first - ⭐ "The pharmacy didn't
   disappear after the handover" is the moment that carries it.
7. **P6 "When I don't get better"** is badged "not part of every journey";
   on the cash side it is the visit plus the new script riding the same
   rails, with no moment of its own.
8. **∥ scenarios, cash side:** out of stock told before the taxi; the dead
   phone at the counter (ID fallback, punchlist 10(i)); the money isn't
   there today (hold-at-counter with price lock, dignity quiet, 10(g) -
   and learning 10 governs the register).

Phases and moments to carry (cash track of #10): P1 At the doctor (⭐ price
before the gate) · P2 Choosing how I get it (go-ahead gate; symptom add-on
attach, 10(c)) · P3 Checked and filled ("Every check ran before it left the
building"; the named check is **Sindi** on the cash side - keep her) · P4 In
hand (⭐ "The counter was expecting me") · P5 Taking it ("Medication you
can't take wrong" + ⭐ day-3 check-in) · P6 When I don't get better (no
moment, cash) · ∥ When something breaks (no moment - #10 keeps failure
recovery momentless).

## Decisions for Tamsin (propose, then ask - do not decide alone)

1. **The thread identity.** #11 lives in "Dis-Chem Repeats". Acute is not
   the repeat service - propose a business-account name (suggest plain
   "Dis-Chem Pharmacy") and ask before building.
2. **Which phase mocks first.** Recommend P1 "At the doctor": it is the
   novel mechanic (photo-of-script in, snap-to-price out, much of the scene
   off-channel in the clinic - system pills will have to carry the room) and
   it holds the journey's marquee star. Confirm before scripting.
3. **Whether P1's off-channel weight** (the consult itself) reads better as
   system pills inside one phone or as a short framing paragraph above it.
4. **The acute change footer** (learning 8): wording and cutoff need her
   sign-off - what a one-day order can still change, and until when.
5. Anything the P1 pass surfaces - the #11 precedent is that structural
   questions (there: P3+P4 compression, still parked) get logged in the
   spec's open questions for the full-set review, not resolved unilaterally.
