What retail finance told us

The processes, teams and friction from the session with the medical aid, debtors and bookkeeping teams. It was framed around the Stitch payments POC and ran almost two hours. Drawn from the auto-transcript, so names of systems and people may be slightly off.

11 processes 8 friction themes Employee lens

The short version

  • The hypothesis was wrong. We were told reconciliation is the team's biggest pain. For in-store payments it isn't: "from a store perspective, we definitely get what we need."
  • The real pain sits in four places: online orders, recovering medical aid shortfalls from customers months after the visit, bad data and inconsistent practice at the counter, and one reconciliation bot shared by every finance team.
  • Most of the friction starts upstream. It lands on finance but begins in the dispensary, at the switch or with the scheme. By the time debtors see a case it's about four months old.
  • Stitch in store is a hard sell. The bar is "zero impact plus cost savings". One consolidated bank account won't work, because every store needs its own account for medical aid. Online is where Stitch helps.

Part 1

Processes discussed

Each process in the order the team described it, with who owns it and the systems involved.

Medical aid team

Claiming at the counter

  1. Dispensary captures the script in Unisolv.
  2. Claim goes via a switching house (MediKredit, MediSwitch) or straight to the scheme.
  3. Approved → medicine dispensed.
  4. Any levy or co-payment is posted to the till by article code; the customer pays at the till.

Unisolv → switch → scheme · GK POS for the levy

Medical aid team

Getting paid by schemes

  1. Most schemes pay in about 30 days, some take 60–90; Discovery pays weekly, per item per script.
  2. Scheme sends a remittance (ERA) directly, not via the switch.
  3. A bot (UiPath) matches remittance to bank payment and updates the Remittances Register.
  4. Any mismatch: investigate, phone the scheme, and if the member owes it, transfer to debtors.

Unisolv "claim settlement" → nightly feed to SAP · Bricks will replace Unisolv

Medical aid team · Dispensary managers

Fixing failed claims

  1. A switch timeout drops items, or a whole day's batch never reaches the scheme.
  2. Store can rewrite only within 7 days; after that it's head office, script by script in Unisolv.
  3. A new levy lands on the "script tracking" report.
  4. Dispensary manager must collect it (it's a KPI, with bonus deductions). The customer sees a pop-up on their next visit.

Unisolv script tracking · store dispensary

Medical aid team → Debtors

Rejections, short payments and clawbacks

  1. Scheme statement shows a short payment or rejection (benefit exceeded, MMAP, membership suspended).
  2. Some schemes (Discovery named) reverse claims 3–4 months after paying in full.
  3. Transferred to the member's account, using the scheme's reason code.
  4. Debtors try to recover from the customer.

Unisolv → SAP business-partner (BP) account

Debtors

Account customers ("standard debtors")

  1. Account set up at head office: old age homes, a university, corporates, legacy individuals.
  2. Customer swipes their loyalty card at any store and buys on account up to a limit (about R500–R10,000 for individuals).
  3. Monthly statement by email; debtors collect.
  4. No new individual accounts: the push is to prepaid.

SAP BP number · GK POS

Debtors

Collecting from customers

  1. Trace the customer (contact details are often stale) and phone them.
  2. Customer pays to a Standard Bank public beneficiary, in store, or by Zapper QR on the statement.
  3. Structured payment plans where needed.
  4. Delinquent accounts: 10 working days' legal notice, credit limit revoked, handed over.

Call management system · bank · store till

Debtors · Online

Online orders

  1. Customer pays online (PayGate); the money sits as a credit on their account.
  2. Team manually allocates sales order numbers to payments.
  3. Goods issue at the store clears the credit, unless the online and SAP prices differ.
  4. PayGate settles in bulk, so one large deposit has to be unpacked into orders.

Magento · PayGate · SAP · Stitch being tested on smaller in-house sites

Bookkeepers

Store reconciliation

  1. Each store has its own bank accounts: Absa for cash, Standard Bank for cards and medical aid.
  2. Bank statements import into SAP automatically; each one splits into 6–8 sub-accounts (cash, cards, medical aid, debtors, credit providers).
  3. A bot exports these by tender to Excel; each bookkeeper covers about 14 stores.
  4. Expected (sales) matched to received (bank), mostly at month end.

SAP GL + sub-accounts + control accounts · BI card report

Stores · Bookkeepers

Taking card and credit payments

  1. GK POS calls a payment switch, which asks the bank or credit provider to approve.
  2. Dual acquiring (Absa and Standard Bank), so a bank outage doesn't stop trading.
  3. The switch's reports feed a daily store "workbench" of matched and unmatched transactions.
  4. Non-bank credit providers (RCS and others) settle on 30/60-day cycles. Duplicate debits are queried through a support provider.

GK POS → payment switch → banks · offline card machines post separately

Stores · Finance

Cash

  1. Till → safe → deposit machine → bank (Absa).
  2. The deposit machine posts into GK, then a control account, then the store sub-account.
  3. That last step often fails, so finance does manual journals.
  4. Fidelity is replacing the deposit machines.

Depositor → GK control account → SAP

Stores · Debtors

Deliveries and paying on delivery

  1. Online orders and phoned-in repeat scripts leave the store with a goods-issue note or script copy.
  2. Payment by Ozow link, or a driver with a card machine.
  3. The proof-of-delivery (POD) should come back so the order can be invoiced and the credit cleared.
  4. How the driver knows the payment cleared is unclear, even to the room.

Ozow · POD slips · Fiori being explored

Edges mentioned

Smaller flows

  1. Tax certificates: customers ask for printouts of private scripts (also logged through Unisolv) before 28 Feb.
  2. Foreign scheme payments (e.g. Namibia): release notifications have stopped arriving.
  3. Fraud claims are investigated with the scheme and the store.
  4. Insurance premiums (Dis-Chem Health and Life): not this team; they run their own accounting.

Unisolv · Treasury · Dis-Chem Health / Life

Part 3

Friction areas

Grouped into eight themes, roughly in order of how much weight the room gave them. Tags show who feels each one.

customer store finance

1 · Recovering money from customers after the visit

The biggest pain for debtors, and the one the customer feels most.

  • Bills arrive months later. A claim approved at the counter can be reversed or short-paid 3–4 months on. The customer gets a call about something they thought was settled.
    "What is the point of us trying to collect something that happened four months ago?"
    customerfinance
  • No reason the customer can act on. Statements often give no reason, or a code. Customers want proof from their medical aid, not from Dis-Chem.
    "Tell me in human language why you're rejecting."
    customerfinance
  • Can't reach the customer. Contact details are out of date, and collection calls are flagged as spam or fraud.
    finance
  • The till doesn't show what's owed. The GK POS debtor pop-up was effectively replaced by a Better Rewards prompt. Cashiers miss balances, and customers who come in to pay are told there's nothing owing.
    customerstore
  • Recovery rates are so low the head floated writing off small medical aid rejections. A "global message" on Unisolv profiles is a stop-gap and can stay up after the customer pays.
    finance
  • Older account holders don't use the newer payment options (e.g. the QR code on the statement). They're used to sorting it out with "their" store.
    customer

2 · Claims that fail between the counter and the scheme

The customer sees "approved", but the money never arrives.

  • Switch timeouts drop items. One item falls off a script that showed as approved; it happens "quite often", currently with Prime Cure.
    financecustomer
  • Whole days go missing. One day's claims (about 200 scripts) never reached ProfMed via MediKredit. MediKredit doesn't warn of outages; MediSwitch does.
    finance
  • No capacity to reprocess. Stores can rewrite only for 7 days; after that it's head office, one script at a time.
    financestore
  • Surprise levies. Reprocessing creates a levy the customer first hears about on their next visit, and only if the dispenser mentions the pop-up.
    "I was here yesterday and there wasn't a levy."
    customerstore
  • Slow and clawback-prone schemes. Some pay in 60–90 days; some reverse full payments months later.
    finance

3 · Data and practice at the counter

"Operations" came up again and again: problems created at the source that finance can't fix.

  • Wrong profile selected. Dispensers pick the wrong person from a date-of-birth match; orders and savings go to the wrong member.
    customerstore
  • Money posts to the wrong account. A daughter's number sits on her mother's profile; an account was even created for a one-year-old.
    finance
  • Fraud. 187 fraud claims reported this year to date, including syndicates using copied IDs and medical aid cards on WhatsApp orders just before closing.
    storefinance
  • Every store does it differently. Card and ID aren't consistently asked for.
    "Each Dis-Chem does things their own way."
    store

4 · No proof to recover against

  • Unsigned scripts and PODs signed by the driver or staff, not the customer, leave debtors with nothing to show. Camera footage works but takes time. The team suggested a QR code at handover so the customer owns who receives.
    financestore
  • Paying on delivery is a blind spot. Nobody in the room could say how the driver knows an Ozow payment cleared, given the distance and time between dispensary, dispatch and doorstep.
    storecustomer

5 · Online orders

  • Never reconciled end to end. Prices differ between Magento and SAP, so balances are left over. Sales order numbers are allocated by hand. PayGate settles in bulk.
    "Reconciling the online store has been impossible."
    finance
  • Online credit leaks into store. An online payment shows as credit the customer can spend at the till before the order is fulfilled.
    financestore

6 · One bot for everyone

  • Shared capacity blocks the day. On Fridays (Discovery payments) teams wait until about 11am to start. Month-end workings are delayed, small changes break the bank recon run, and each new scheme slows it. Momentum's two payments against one remittance still need adding.
    "There's one bot that is being shared amongst multiple finance [teams]."
    finance
  • Improvement ideas are stuck for lack of bot capacity.
    finance

7 · Bookkeeping gaps (smaller)

  • Card report shows totals only. There's no Visa/Mastercard split, so differences are hard to pinpoint. BI is adding it, with SnapScan, Zapper and credit providers to follow.
    finance
  • Cash postings fail. Deposit-machine postings often don't reach the sub-account, so journals are done by hand. Fidelity may fix this.
    finance
  • Bank statement imports are sometimes late, and nothing can be reconciled until they land.
    finance

8 · Odd ones out

  • Foreign scheme payments stuck. Namibian scheme payments aren't being released since a notification process changed.
    finance
  • Tax-season printouts. A rush of requests for proof of private-script spend ahead of 28 Feb.
    storecustomer

Context

Where the room landed on Stitch

QuestionWhat the room said
Is in-store payment broken?No. The current switch gives them what they need, and replacing it "actually scares me".
One bank account for all stores?Non-starter. Each store's pharmacy licence needs its own account for medical aid payments; a team of about 37 is organised around per-store recon; JV stores and Baby City are separate legal entities.
Risks raisedReliability in physical stores (banks used to fall over on Mondays); Stitch settles a day later where the bank settles same day.
Where it helpsOnline: order-to-settlement visibility was shown in testing. Going live first on smaller in-house sites.
Bar for an in-store POC"Zero impact plus cost savings." Possibly faster sight of duplicate debits, but that's a very small part of their day.
