Start here

Every sale splits into money now and money later

If you keep one thing in your head tomorrow, keep this. It explains why finance has a whole team for medical aid and debtors, and why "recon" takes so much effort.

Money now

What the customer pays at the till: card, cash, a Mobicred code, a voucher. The bank pays it into Dis-Chem's account within a day or two. It's fairly certain and easy to check.

Money later

What a medical aid (or an insurer) has promised to pay. It comes weeks later in a batch, with a statement listing which claims it covers. Sometimes less arrives than was promised. Someone has to spot that and chase it.

A cash customer buying sunscreen is all "now". A medical aid member collecting chronic medicine is mostly "later", plus a small "now" if there's a co-payment. The clinic and the dispensary produce most of the "later".

Part 1

End to end: from the customer to a reconciled book

First, where customers pay today in a Store of the Future. Then the five steps every rand goes through after that.

Front shop

Scan and pay. The Better Rewards discount comes off instantly at the till: 10%, plus extra for paying with a linked Capitec card or after a pharmacy purchase.

Pays at: front-shop till

Dispensary

The pharmacy asks the medical aid electronically and gets an answer in seconds: paid, paid with a co-payment, or rejected. Cash patients pay the regulated medicine price.

Pays at: front-shop till (a Magna bag means "pay", a brown bag means nothing due)

Clinic

The nurse consult and the medicine are two separate payments. The cash price is R99 for a nurse consult, R275 with a video doctor. Medical aid can be claimed instead.

Pays at: Health Hub or till, then medicine at the till

Cover

The advisor sells Dis-Chem Health (medical insurance, gap cover) and Dis-Chem Life (life, funeral). Premiums then run by monthly debit order.

Pays at: Hub or advisor by card; then debit order

Worth knowing: SOTF originally wanted payment at the dispensary. It was pushed out to 2028, waiting on a POS system update, so today's customer finishes at the pharmacy and then queues again at the front to pay. Online is different again: Dis-Chem Online doesn't claim from medical aid, so those customers pay and claim back themselves.

  1. The customer pays or claims

    At the till, each sale records how it was paid: card, cash, Mobicred code, gift card, Discovery Miles voucher. For medicine, the pharmacy system has already sent the claim to the medical aid through a middleman called a switch, and the switch's answer says how much the scheme will pay and how much the member owes.

    member's share → paid nowscheme's share → owed later

  2. The store closes the day

    Each till is "cashed up": what the system says was taken is checked against what's actually in the drawer and the card machine. Cash goes to the bank via a smart safe or a cash-in-transit collection. Any difference is flagged to head office.

    cash and card totals

  3. The bank pays in card money

    The card machine sends the day's batch to Dis-Chem's bank (the "acquirer"). It pays the money into Dis-Chem's account a day or two later, minus a small fee. Mobicred, vouchers and Discovery Miles each settle through their own route.

    1–2 days

  4. The medical aids pay in batches

    By law a scheme must pay within 30 days of getting a claim. It pays many claims in one go and sends a remittance listing what each payment covers. If a claim is wrong, the scheme must say why within 30 days, and Dis-Chem has 60 days to fix and resend it. Claims older than about four months can be refused.

    up to 30 daysshort payments and rejections

  5. Finance reconciles everything

    "Recon" means matching every rand received to the sale it belongs to. It covers card money against card sales, cash deposits against cash-ups, and remittances against claims, line by line. Whatever doesn't match gets chased or written off. Medical aids can also take back money they paid in error, straight out of a later payment, so a batch can arrive lower than expected.

    remittance vs claimsclawbacks, reversals, refunds

Two quieter money flows are probably in the mix too. Brand-funded discounts: if suppliers pay for some Better Rewards discounts, finance has to claim that back from them. Insurance commission: Dis-Chem earns fees from the insurers behind Dis-Chem Health and Life. Both are guesses to check.

Part 2

The players

Who touches the money on the way through. likely marks roles I'd expect to exist but haven't confirmed.

Inside Dis-Chem

  • Cashiers at front-shop tillsTake almost every in-store payment, including for scripts in SOTF stores.
  • Pharmacists and dispensary staffCapture the script and send the claim. They're first to see a rejection or co-payment.
  • Clinic nurses and Clinic Care AssistantsDeliver the service that gets billed as cash or to the medical aid.
  • Health Hub AdministratorTakes clinic and cover payments and handles queries at the Hub.
  • Cover advisorSells insurance in store; takes card payment.
  • Store managerOwns the daily cash-up and till differences.likely
  • Retail finance (tomorrow's room)Head of retail finance, the medical aid manager and the debtors managers: they get the money in, match it and chase what's missing.
  • Treasury / cash managementBank relationships, cash-in-transit, card fees.likely
  • Better Rewards / loyalty teamSets the discounts that finance then has to account for.
  • IT and systemsThe till system (GKPOS), dispensing (Unisolv), clinic records (Healthforce), finance (SAP).
  • Dis-Chem Health and Dis-Chem LifeThe insurance arms, run with partner insurers.
  • TLC and wholesale financeCollect from franchise and independent pharmacies. Probably a separate team.

Outside Dis-Chem

  • CustomersCash shoppers, medical aid members, policyholders.
  • Medical schemesDiscovery, GEMS, Bonitas, Medshield and others. Dis-Chem is a preferred ("network") pharmacy for several, including Discovery, GEMS, POLMED, Fedhealth and Medihelp.
  • Scheme administratorsThe companies that run schemes day to day and actually pay the claims, such as Discovery Health, Medscheme and Momentum.
  • Claims switchesThe middlemen that pass claims from the pharmacy to the scheme in seconds: MediKredit, SwitchOn and Healthbridge. Which one Dis-Chem uses isn't public.
  • Banks and card acquirersTurn card taps into money in the account, for a fee.
  • Card schemesVisa and Mastercard set the rules, including customer disputes (chargebacks).
  • Payment partnersCapitec (reward boost), Mobicred and RCS (credit), Discovery Miles, gift-card providers.
  • Cash-in-transit companiesCollect cash or empty the smart safes.likely
  • Insurers behind Dis-Chem's coverCentriq (Dis-Chem Health), administered by Kaelo; Guardrisk (funeral); OneSpark (life). Dis-Chem owns stakes in Kaelo and OneSpark.
  • Brand suppliersMay fund part of the Better Rewards discounts.likely
  • RegulatorsThe Council for Medical Schemes (claims rules, and a January 2026 ruling on Dis-Chem Health's branding), the Department of Health (medicine prices), the Reserve Bank (payments).

"Debtors" simply means anyone who owes Dis-Chem money after the customer has left. Dis-Chem reported about R3.6bn owed to it in August 2025, but the public numbers don't show how much of that sits with medical aids and how much with franchisees, suppliers or insurers.

Friction to test

Where it probably hurts

Hypotheses from the research and the SOTF launch documents. Use them as prompts. Tomorrow will tell you which are real and which are missing.

Customer · store

A second queue to pay

In SOTF stores the customer collects at the pharmacy, then pays at the front till. Paying at the dispensary is on hold until 2028.

Customer · pharmacist

Surprises at the counter

A co-payment or rejection shows up only when the claim is sent, often at the counter with the customer waiting. "Approved" doesn't always mean "paid in full".

Customer · Hub

Two payments for one clinic visit

The consult and the medicine are paid separately, at different points.

Customer · finance

Paid twice, refunded slowly

Complaints describe paying cash while the pharmacy also claimed from medical aid, or cash items put on medical aid. Refunds then take a long time.

Finance

Money that doesn't match

Batches arrive short, lines get rejected, and schemes take back earlier overpayments out of later payments. Each gap needs someone to find and chase it.

Finance

Many payment types, many trails

Card, cash, Mobicred, vouchers, Discovery Miles, stacked reward discounts: each settles differently and needs its own matching.

Store · customer

When systems go down

If the switch, the till or connectivity drops (load-shedding included), claims can't go through, and customers pay cash or wait.

Customer

Online can't claim

Online buyers pay in full and claim back themselves. That's a gap as SOTF joins online and in-store.

Cheat sheet

Words you'll hear

Switch
The electronic middleman that sends a claim from the pharmacy to the medical aid and brings the answer back in seconds.
Claim / adjudication
Asking the medical aid to pay; adjudication is the medical aid deciding how much.
Co-payment / levy
The part the member pays themselves, e.g. because the medicine isn't on the scheme's list or they didn't use a network pharmacy.
Remittance (ERA)
The statement a medical aid sends with a payment, listing which claims it covers.
Recon
Reconciliation: matching money received to the sales and claims it belongs to.
Short payment
The scheme paid less than was claimed.
Rejection / resubmission
The scheme refused a claim; fixing it and sending it again.
Reversal
Cancelling a claim that was already sent, e.g. a script that was never collected.
Clawback / recovery
The scheme taking back money it paid in error, out of a later payment.
Unallocated payment
Money that arrived but can't yet be matched to anything.
Ageing / debtors' book
How long money has been owed, usually in 30, 60, 90 and 120-day buckets. Older is worse.
Write-off
Giving up on money that won't be collected.
Tender
How a customer paid: cash, card, voucher, and so on.
Acquirer / settlement
The bank that processes card payments / the moment the money actually lands in the account.
Chargeback
A card customer disputing a payment through their bank.
DSP / network pharmacy
A pharmacy a medical aid has chosen as preferred. Members who go elsewhere may pay more.
SEP + dispensing fee
The legally controlled medicine price plus the capped fee the pharmacy may add.
NAPPI / ICD-10
The product code for the medicine and the diagnosis code. Both go on the claim, and mistakes cause rejections.
Practice number
The registration number every provider bills under, pharmacies and clinics included.
PMB / chronic authorisation
Conditions schemes must cover, and the approval a member needs before chronic medicine is paid from the right benefit.

For the room

Questions for tomorrow

Grouped by the three things you want to leave with. Start broad: ask them to walk you through it before you test any of the above.

The process

  1. Can you walk me through what happens to one medical aid script, from the claim at the counter to the money being matched in your books?Then the same for a cash front-shop sale and a clinic visit.
  2. Where does the process break most often, and where do you spend the most time fixing things by hand?
  3. What happens when a claim is rejected or short-paid after the customer has already left?
  4. How do refunds work for something that was claimed from medical aid?
  5. What changed for you when Better Rewards launched? Who pays for each layer of discount, and how does that get settled?

The players

  1. Who's in your team and what does each person own? Who do you depend on in stores, IT and the pharmacy?
  2. Which medical aids, administrators and switches do you deal with most, and which are hardest to work with?
  3. How do Dis-Chem Health and Dis-Chem Life show up in your world: commission, claims paid to our clinics, or both?

The customers

  1. What do you call the different types of customer you process payments for? Which are the biggest by value, and which cause the most work?
  2. Do we have account customers (care homes, companies, doctors, staff)? How are they set up, billed and chased?
  3. If you could change one thing about how customers pay in store, what would it be?Good closer. It hands you their own future-state wish.
