Thabo's acute script, as a WhatsApp conversation

The cash track of the acute journey (#10), rendered as the literal WhatsApp thread on Thabo's phone across one sick day - from the photo of the paper script in the clinic queue to the day-3 check-in that turns into a booked appointment. Every message uses a real mechanic of the WhatsApp Business Platform, and every mechanic is chosen to honour his constraints: prepaid data, an entry-level Android, no app, no account, a sick 6-year-old at his side. The chat lives in Dis-Chem Pharmacy - not the repeat service; his entry is a photo, not a subscription. The phones are live: tap the highlighted buttons and dashed pills to walk each conversation. What happens away from the phone - the clinic room, the express counter, the pharmacist's call - is told beside the phone, never manufactured as chat; P4 carries no phone at all, because its whole scene is a counter. The threads render here in English for this audience; production renders them in the customer's chosen language.

Trust > Convenience > Speed  - speed as evidence of care: the burden moves, that's the promise
Caregiver · his son's illness · cash

Thabo Mokoena

31 · Tembisa · uninsured, pays cash at the margin · WhatsApp on prepaid data is his whole internet. Today: his 6-year-old's chest infection, and a paper script from the clinic.

The need
“Just tell me the price on WhatsApp. If it's too much, show me a cheaper one that works - I'm not made of money and my child is sick now, not next week.”

The thread starts where the channel genuinely enters: in the queue to pay, when Thabo photographs the script and sends it to the number on the clinic's poster.

What each message is doing

markers (B1-B5) match the callouts · A0 applies to every phase on this page · ⚠️ marks claims awaiting a vault source

A0
Language · standing note
Production renders this thread in the language Thabo chooses. There is no sign-up on the acute path, so where that choice happens is an open question in the spec - English is shown here for this audience only.
B1
Outgoing media message · the whole front door
The entry is a photo, not a subscription: no account, no app, no forms - a number from the clinic poster and the script he was already holding. The journey's one media message travels in the direction he chose to spend; everything the service sends back is text, because on prepaid every download costs real money.
B2
Text ack · proof it arrived
The received-confirmation beat: today the hand-off is the patient's problem, with no way to tell a script arrived. One immediate line kills that pain - and "you don't need to reply or stay online" is written for a prepaid balance that can lapse without losing his place.
B3
Read-back · so he can check it's right
The script read back in plain words, with his son's weight from the script shown - because a child's dose is checked against weight before anything is made up. The paper in his pocket stops being the only copy that matters.
B4
Price before travel · inside 15 minutes
The full price in rand, itemised, with the generic saving shown exactly - offered, never asked for. The dignity thread of #10 runs through this message: the generic is the smart choice, and nobody at a counter hears the question. The 15-minute snap-to-price reply is a design assumption ⚠️; amounts are illustrative ⚠️.
Key moment⭐ moment of truthat B4
I knew the price before I left the clinic gate
Thabo has done this trip before, the old way: the taxi to a pharmacy with the week's money in his pocket and no idea if it's enough, the queue with a sick child, the counter where the number is finally said out loud - and the walk away, in front of everyone, if it's too much. This time the number came to him, standing at the clinic, before anything was spent. He knows the price - and that he can afford it - before his son takes another step. Nobody made him ask; the price was simply offered, with the cheaper generic presented as the smart choice it is. That is Snap-a-Script meeting him exactly where he stands - one photo, on the phone he already has.
B5
Interactive reply buttons · max 3
The choice is his, in one tap: the generic, the branded as written, or a person. The branded option stays real - it plays a price, not a lecture - and "Talk to a person" ends in a free phone call in his language, not a chat queue ⚠️. Nothing expires while he thinks, and nothing is made up in his son's name until his go-ahead lands - that gate is P2's job.

What each message is doing

C1
Route ask · typed answer first-class
Three honest ways to answer, none of them a form: name the store, share a location to see stores near where he stands, or an address only if he wants a delivery cost - the two never conflated. The reply confirms his store has the medicine before he commits, because the routing ask and the stock answer are the same step ⚠️.
C2
"1 - go ahead" · the release gate
The journey's one mid-flow confirmation, and the message says why it exists: nothing is made up in his son's name until this lands - the legal and operational release (#10 P2 rule), the acute counterpart of #11's sign-up read-back. Nothing starts without it, and nothing expires while he thinks.
C3
Confirmation · everything the day needs
Express pickup zone, ready from this afternoon, held until closing, and the after-hours point on the same code if the shift runs late ⚠️. The whole plan is on the phone before he goes back to work.
C4
Numbered symptom add-ons · silence adds nothing
Diagnosis-led suggestions for a chest like his, each priced, answered with one digit - and the total is restated the moment anything changes, so price-before-travel survives the additions ⚠️. Add-ons never gate the medicine; if he says nothing, nothing is added.

No key moment in this phase by design - #10 keeps P2 deliberately quiet. Dis-Chem runs ahead of the customer: preparation starts at the go-ahead, and the medicine is ready before he is.

What each message is doing

markers (D1-D4) match the callouts

D1
Status template · detail inline
The status arrives without being asked and carries its content in the message - each item scanned against the doctor's script - never "open a link to see more", because a link is a download and a download is money. He's at work; he doesn't have to watch it, but it's there when he looks.
D2
The named check · for his weight
The pharmacist is named only at the final check (the #10 name rule), and the check that gets named is the one a parent cares about: the dose against his son's weight, off the script. Her line stays open in the same thread - no phoning a dispensary that doesn't pick up.
Key momentat D2
Every check ran before it left the building
For a child's medicine, a mistake isn't an inconvenience - it's the thing a parent lies awake about. Today that safety runs invisibly, and any gap shows up at home, too late. Here it runs while Thabo works his shift, and it shows itself: picked at his store, scanned against the doctor's script, and a named pharmacist confirming the dose against his son's weight before signing. Someone with a name stood behind his boy's medicine - and he watched it happen. That is The Named Check, made for the person with the most to lose from an anonymous mistake.
D3
Ready + the order code as text
Ready before the shift ends, with the code as a plain text line the counter can key or scan - not a barcode image to download. The same code works at the after-hours point outside the store ⚠️. (#10 speaks of a QR at the express counter; the text render keeps the service's downloads at zero - flagged in the spec's open questions.)
D4
The dosing guide, ahead of his hands
His son's dose in his language, on the phone before the medicine is in them - so the counter walkthrough confirms what he's already read instead of introducing it. The same instructions ride on the labels.

Everything Thabo needs - the code, the total, the dosing guide - has been on his phone since 15:05. The scene below is the approved #10 copy, told where it happens: at the counter.

off-channel · the express counter
E1

After his shift Thabo stops at his store. The express pickup zone is signposted right at the front - the dispensary queue is somebody else's afternoon. He shows his code, and the pack comes out with his son's name on it. No forms, no retelling the story.

E2

The pharmacist at the counter opens the pack with him: the syringe marked at his line, the labels in his language, finish all seven days even when he seems better. He asks what he wants to ask while he's standing there.

E3

One total - the script and the paracetamol syrup - the same number he's known since the clinic: R208.90. Cash or card, at the same counter, and done.

The thread stays silent through all of it - and nothing needs to be sent. If his phone had died on the way, the ∥ band covers that: his ID is enough.

What the counter is doing

markers (E1-E3) match the beats in the panel

E1
The code at handover
The code he shows is the E-Script Token's redemption face: his son's whole script riding one code, redeemable exactly once, by him. The pack was staged under name and code when it sealed ⚠️.
Key moment⭐ moment of truthat E1
The counter was expecting me
Thabo knows the other version of this stop: the month-end dispensary queue, twenty-five minutes deep, after a full shift, with a sick boy waiting at home. This time the counter was expecting him - the pack made up, named, waiting - and he was through it in minutes. The stop still happens; it just stopped costing him an afternoon he didn't have. That is The 60-Minute Fill shaped for collection - and the code he showed is The E-Script Token doing its quiet work: his son's whole script riding one code, redeemable exactly once, by him.
E2
Counselling worked into the handover
The walkthrough is part of the handover, not a queue away from it - the duty pharmacist at the counter, the marked syringe, the in-language labels. (Sindi, who signed the check, stays reachable in the thread - the #10 agreement.)
E3
Pay what he already knew
The number planned at the clinic gate is the number paid at the counter - one amount, once, cash or card. Paying cash is a completely normal way to buy medicine here; nothing about this counter treats it as an error state.

Whether P3 and P4 want to share one phone on this surface (the ready message is the last thing the channel says before the counter) is parked in the spec's open questions for this review.

Taking it

that evening, then day 3 · Fri 11 Sep

Key momentoff-channel · the first dose
Medication you can't take wrong
Dosing a sick child from instructions in your second language is a quiet, common danger - and tonight it isn't one. The syringe is marked, the words are his father's own language, the pictures carry what words might miss. Getting it right required nothing from Thabo except being there. That is Made-for-Me Medicine at its free floor - the can't-take-wrong ladder that comes with every script, no matter who is paying.
F1
The day-3 check-in · the single touch
One message, timed from the course profile, answered in one tap - and it is the only thing this journey ever pushes. No dose reminders, no course-end message, nothing unasked (#10 phase decision): the research is blunt that help flips into harassment the moment agency is lost.
F2
Escalation · a person, by phone
When the answer is "he isn't better", the next step is a voice, not a chatbot - a pharmacist phones after his shift, in his language, free, and his data can lapse because the call comes to him. The call itself happens off the channel; the thread holds only its arrangement and its outcome.
Key moment⭐ moment of truthat F1-F3
The pharmacy didn't disappear after the handover
Nobody has ever asked Thabo whether his child's medicine is working - day three is where every parent is alone with their judgement. This time day three brought a question in his own language, and when his answer was "no", it didn't bounce off a chatbot: a pharmacist phoned, listened, and turned his worry into a booked appointment with a price he knew first. Someone asked if his boy was getting better - and when he wasn't, they did something about it. That is The Pharmacist Who Stays earning its name: the check-in that becomes a plan the moment the answer is wrong.
F3
The booking, in writing, price first
The pharmacist gave the price on the phone before Thabo said yes; the written confirmation restates it so the plan lives on the phone, not in his memory of a call ⚠️. The week's story travels with the booking - he will not retell it at the clinic.

What each message is doing

markers (H1-H3) match the callouts · scenarios, not steps - each is a different version of the day

H1
Exception template · the plan attached
Bad news never arrives alone: the stock failure reaches him before a rand goes to a taxi, carrying its two ways through as buttons - the next-nearest store on the same code and price, or delivery with its cost shown honestly ⚠️. The wasted trip is the one thing never allowed to happen.
H2
"Hold it for me" · price locked
When the week costs more than it should, one reply holds the pack at the counter with the price unchanged - no re-order, no questions on the day he comes, and nobody at that counter knows anything except that a pack is waiting for its person ⚠️.
H3
The dead phone · no thread presence
A twelve-hour shift kills a battery. There is deliberately no message to show for it: at the express counter his ID is enough - the pack is under his son's name, and the order is his, not his phone's ⚠️. The code was for speed, never a lock.

No key moment, by #10 design - the scenarios carry the band. Every one ends with Thabo holding the decision, and bad news always arrives with the plan attached.
