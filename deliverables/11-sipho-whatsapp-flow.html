Sipho's repeat, as a WhatsApp conversation

The cash track of the household repeat journey (#09), rendered as the literal WhatsApp thread on Sipho's phone - one phone per phase, from sign-up in July through a full August cycle to the months when something breaks. Every message uses a real mechanic of the WhatsApp Business Platform - templates, reply buttons, list sheets, a Flow, voice notes - and every mechanic is chosen to honour his constraints: prepaid data, an entry-level Android, no app, no street address. The phones are live: tap the highlighted rows and dashed pills to walk each conversation. The rail alongside each phone explains what the messages are doing and why. The threads render here in English for this audience; in production they arrive in the language Sipho chose at sign-up.

Trust > Convenience > Speed  - predictability beats speed
Patient · household medicine manager · cash

Sipho

45 · peri-urban Durban · informal income · hypertensive, on a cheap Android with intermittent signal, no street address · collects for his elderly mother (chronic, CCMDD) - cash, at the margin, near home.

The need
Manage the family's medicine with whatever phone and airtime he has - clear cash price up front, collect near home, dignity intact, no app required.

What each message is doing

markers (B1-B5) match the callouts · A0 applies to every phase on this page

A0
Language · standing note
Production renders every thread on this page in the language Sipho picks in this phase, with his mother's medicine instructions in hers. English is shown here for this audience only.
B1
Invite template · the origin story
The entry is an invitation: after an in-store collection, the service message arrives with the value proposition as its headline - which is also what the notification preview shows, so the hook lands before the chat is even opened. The pharmacist who mentioned it stays unnamed; a poster QR remains the alternative route in. Sign-up starts with one tap, no app store, no account.
B2
List message · language first
Before anything else is asked, the thread's language becomes his - one sheet, one tap, six languages.
B3
WhatsApp Flow · native form
The one place a form beats chat: household and medicines on a native sheet, no webview, no data-heavy page. His row is a dispensing record - plain handle, molecule and strength, monthly quantity. Her row is described by origin (the programme fills and labels her parcel), because Dis-Chem never opens what it never dispenses. Consent is an already-granted fact whose scope is read back - order, collect, manage - never an in-flow event (POPIA: recorded once, in-language, auditable), and her medicine-list share is her own revocable grant.
B4
The date-move exchange · one decision per turn
Script Sync made visible, then repurposed: the sync offer, its cost, the own-day pick and the read-back each arrive as their own turn, exploration labels until the single commitment button. Every price lands before a decision is asked for ⚠️. And the rail carries what the chat never asks: he picks the 28th because it sits after month-end payday - his reasoning, never the service's data.
B5
Read-back + reply-1 confirm
Everything he chose, read back in plain language, confirmed with a single character - the consent gate. The same read-back-then-yes rule guards every later change to money, medicine or dates.
Key momentat B5
I told them once how I want it
One WhatsApp conversation, a few minutes - and it's remembered: not asked again at a counter, not to a driver, not at the bank. The CarerConsent concept, on the phone he already owns.

What each message is doing

A1
Utility template · business-initiated
The one-week-out notice: his order only - her programme parcel landed on Tuesday and is tracked, never managed. No image header, no PDF - on a prepaid balance every download costs real money. It names the day (never "payday" - what the 28th means is his business), and it closes on the golden path: nothing to do. The small grey footer under this and every service message is the standing change surface: reply CHANGE, anywhere, any time, until the day before.
Key moment⭐ moment of truthat A1
My medicine ordered itself
He reads it and puts the phone away - nothing to install, nothing to spend, nothing to remember. The family's supply stays ahead of run-out, on the phone he already owns. The Auto-Refill concept with no app in the way.
A2
Formatted price breakdown · in-bubble
The price lives in the message, not at the till - one line per item, emoji as anchors (rung 1 of the visual ladder: characters, not downloads). His tablets and the fee only: there is no CCMDD line, because there is nothing to pay on hers (v3.4). Amounts and product names are illustrative ⚠️.
Key moment⭐ moment of truthat A2
The only thing I ever bought blind - not anymore
He knows whether the trip is affordable before he decides to travel - not at the till, after the taxi fare is already spent. Real-Time Transparency, for the majority who pay cash.
A3
Interactive reply buttons · max 3
WhatsApp allows exactly three quick-reply buttons (sourced: 3 buttons, 20-character titles), and the design spends them on doors: add, change, person. There is no confirm button on purpose - silence is the golden path, and the order carries on by itself. "Add to my order" is P2's doorway: the suggestions arrive only if he knocks. Pause lives inside the change menu now, not on a button.
A4
The one change menu · 9 rows
Behind "Change something" and the CHANGE keyword alike: one list sheet (sourced limit: 10 rows), frequency-ordered - money moves first (date, skip), preference tweaks in the middle (payment, generic or brand), admin then destructive last (personal details, cancel - which ends with a person), and free text always the final row. The date row asks its own follow-up: just this month, or every month. (tap Change something in the phone to see it)
A5
Keyword reply + phone-back
Escalation ends in a phone call, not a chat queue: one reply, and a person phones him in his language, free of charge. The closing line matters most on prepaid - he can let his data lapse, because the call comes to him. (tap Talk to a person in the phone to see it)

What each message is doing

markers (C1-C5) match the callouts

C1
User-pulled · an answer, not an ambush
Nothing here arrives unprompted: he tapped "Add to my order" in the P1 phone, on Saturday morning, when it suited him. The suggestions are a reply because he asked - and his tap opens the 24-hour service window, so none of this needs a marketing template. The honest constraint is stated up front: medicine only, no groceries - and the browse view is scoped the same way, so it cannot un-say it.
C2
Product cards · rung 2 of the visual ladder
Catalogue cards carry the shelf photo, the name, the price and add-to-order in one block. At every literacy level, recognising the box beats reading its name - the thumbnail is an accessibility device, not decoration, and it is the one place the mock spends pixels. No owner is guessed: the suggestions say "from what you usually buy", never whose they are - a till slip has no owner ⚠️.
C3
Browse medicines · the in-WhatsApp catalogue
For the person who'd rather look around than type: the catalogue is WhatsApp's own browsable surface - no website, no webview - and the cart lands back in this thread as an order, where the running total lives. Thumbnails load as he scrolls, so browsing is data he chooses to spend. (tap Browse medicines in the phone)
C4
Free text · one clarifying question
Typing "cough mixture" is as good as any card. The service asks exactly one question back - child or adult - as reply buttons, then returns priced options as cards. For a child, the dose is checked against their weight by a pharmacist, so a parent collects with confidence. Never a form, never a dead end.
C5
Running total · restated every change
Price-before-travel survives the additions: after every item the total he'll pay at the Capitec counter is restated in full ⚠️.

No key moment in this phase by design - #09 agreed the cash addition carries none. The value is quieter: the small pharmacy trips between paydays fold into the one he already makes.

What each message is doing

markers (D1-D4) match the callouts

D1
Status templates · detail inline
Both statuses arrive without being asked, and carry their content in the message itself - never "open a link to see more", because a link is a download and a download is money.
D2
The named check · plain lines
Each line answers "checked what?" in words a customer can carry: right medicine and dose, nothing clashes, the script and his history. The clash line teaches by example - his order really does hold the one interaction a pharmacist watches for (decongestant syrups against blood-pressure tablets). His order only: the programme's parcel is the programme's check (v3.4) - the pharmacist checks what Dis-Chem dispenses plus what the household has shared. The pharmacist is named at the final check - the #10 name rule. (Name is a placeholder - spec open questions.)
D3
Nothing issued at sealing
Packed-and-sealed is a pure status: no PIN. The credential belongs to the moment the parcel is physically in the locker (v3.6) - P4's message first-issues it, because a code on the phone is a promise, and this service only promises what already exists.
D4
CHANGE · used, never offered
Fulfilment flexibility is reply-shaped: no message body offers to change the plan, but the footer stands under every status, and one keyword opens the same menu from anywhere - shift the date if the money moves, switch locker, store or costed delivery, until the day before. The demo branch shows it working; the mock then returns to Friday. (tap the CHANGE pill in the phone to see it)
Key momentat D2
Every check ran before it left the building
The same checks, the same standard, the same named signature. How he pays has no bearing on how carefully his family's medicine is handled - and for the first time, he can see that. The Named Check, identical on the cash rail.

What each message is doing

E1
PIN issued on locker arrival
The message comes the moment the parcel is physically in the locker - the PIN's first issue, not a restatement (v3.6), with the two retrieval steps in order because the counter payment is what activates the PIN. Amount and code travel together, loaded onto the phone before he leaves signal - the whole trip in one message. The chat says "the 28th", never "payday": the day is his, and so is what it means.
E2
Pay-then-PIN · off-channel by design
The handover itself never touches the chat: cash at the Capitec counter releases the locker on his PIN - the Pelebox pattern near home, no street address required. The partner-counter release is a design assumption ⚠️. In-chat payment is deliberately absent: WhatsApp payments aren't live in South Africa, and cash needs no channel.
E3
Photo reply -> a person phones
The contents list lands right after handover so the parcel is checked at the locker, not at home after the taxi. A photo in the same thread brings a person to the phone at once ⚠️. (tap the photo pill in the phone to see it)
Key moment⭐ moment of truthat E2
I could track it from Dis-Chem's door to mine
The same certainty, differently shaped: payment and collection in one visit, near home, with no address required - and if the locker is ever down, he knows before he travels, not after.

What each message is doing

markers (F1-F4) match the callouts

F1
List message · the medicine cabinet
Every medicine the family takes, one sheet away, by its real name - so phone, pack and shelf match word for word. The cabinet convention: dispensed medicine carries its owner, her clinic medicines appear because she granted the see-dial of CarerConsent ("from her clinic script" is the provenance - a told-us record the pharmacist can use but never vouches for), and OTC purchases are (Household) unless someone assigns them - the service never guesses. Product names illustrative ⚠️.
F2
Voice notes · both directions
When reading isn't enough, he asks in the thread - typed or spoken. Voice carries the literacy range, and it's his mother's channel as much as his: her reminders arrive on her phone as short voice notes in her language.
F3
STOP · honoured instantly
One keyword ends the reminders, completely and immediately. The research is blunt about why: the same tools that reduce effort flip into harassment the moment agency is lost.
F4
The free floor lives in the parcel
Iconised labels, the printed day-by-day fridge sheet, instructions in each person's language - the adherence basics ride with the medicine, not the phone. The chat's dose lines mirror the pack's icons (🌅 🍽 ✔), so the two surfaces speak the same visual language; the chat carries only the look-up and the pharmacist.
Key momentat F4
Medication you can't take wrong
The basics done properly: a clear label on every pack, icons that speak before words do, a sheet on the fridge, a guide on the phone. Taking it right isn't something money buys - it comes with the medicine. The promise Made-for-Me Medicine makes at the paid end, kept at the free end too.

What each message is doing

markers (G1-G4) match the callouts · scenarios, not steps - each is a different month

G1
Exception template · the plan attached
Bad news never arrives alone: the message carries the live choices as reply buttons, and nothing is decided without him. An exception opens a path - it never kills the order. His order only: a stock-out never touches the programme parcel, so the split offers "the rest of your order", not her medicine (v3.4).
G2
Told before travelling
The taxi-fare rule: anything knowable before he travels - a broken locker, a stock-out, a waiting renewal - is told before he travels. His fare is never spent on a closed door.
G3
Script Renew · his own script
The script that runs out is his - his mother's belongs to the clinic and its programme (v3.4). The renewal starts weeks before it runs dry, visibly, in the same thread, and chasing it costs him nothing. The full renewal flow is its own journey (the Script Renew concept), out of scope here.
G4
A person, by phone
When things truly break, the human comes by phone, one reply away, in his language - the loud human-not-bot beat the sentiment research demands.

No key moment on the cash track by design: #09's ∥ moment of truth is medical-aid-specific and insured-only. The cash recovery paths carry the same care without a separate memory line.
