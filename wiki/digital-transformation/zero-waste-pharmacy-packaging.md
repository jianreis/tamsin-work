# Zero-waste pharmacy packaging: returnable, recyclable and cold-chain precedents

There is a real precedent for a returnable outer container on a home-delivered
pharmacy service, and none at all for reusing the pack that actually holds the
medicine. Walmart Specialty Pharmacy shipped cold-chain prescriptions to
patients in a reconditioned insulated box that came back through the carrier
network and was reused 20 to 55 times [[src-greenbox-walmart-specialty-reusable-shipper]].
Checkers Sixty60 drivers already collect used delivery bags at the door on the
next drop, in South Africa, today [[src-sixty60-bag-recycling-shoprite]].
Amazon's PillPack, the model for Made-for-Me Medicine, ships PET sachets in a
recyclable cardboard dispenser and offers a reusable dispenser the customer
keeps, but takes nothing back [[src-pillpack-packaging-help]]. Every pharmacy
scheme that does take packaging back (Boots, Superdrug, Pharmacycle, Chiesi
Take AIR) recycles or destroys it; none refills it. So Dis-Chem's version is a
durable, Dis-Chem-owned dispenser box collected and swapped by the driver each
month, with the sachet roll inside treated as single-use consumable, and the
cold-chain leg handled by a returnable cool box on the same swap. That is
legal and operable now. Reusing a sachet, tray or bottle that has held a
patient's dispensed medicine is not, anywhere we found.

## Retail returnable-packaging precedents

Sixty60's scheme is a recycling loop with a driver-collected return leg.
Customers flat-pack up to ten used paper bags inside one Sixty60 bag and leave
it at the door when the next delivery is due; the driver takes it back to the
store for recycling. It trialled in late January 2021 and rolled out nationally
from March 2021 as part of Shoprite's zero-waste-to-landfill ambition
[[src-sixty60-bag-recycling-shoprite]]. The bags are pulped, not refilled, but
the customer behaviour Dis-Chem needs (leave the empty out, driver swaps it) is
already trained into the SA market by the country's largest on-demand grocer.

Loop, TerraCycle's deposit-return platform, is the closest thing to a
refill-and-return consumer packaging system at retail scale. Branded goods ship
in durable containers against a refundable deposit, empties go into a tote for
pickup or into a store drop-off, and the containers are washed and refilled.
Kroger and Walgreens (US), Carrefour, Aeon, Tesco and Woolworths Australia all
carried it, and Woolworths' version included home collection of the tote
[[src-loop-terracycle-retail-reuse]]. The record is mixed. Loop shut its
standalone home-delivery model and finished 2022 with about 150 retail
locations worldwide [[src-loop-terracycle-retail-reuse]]. Tesco, its biggest
grocery partner, ended the trial in mid-2022 after twelve months online and nine
in ten stores, with roughly 80,000 products sold, and reported that "pre-fill"
(the customer collects a container someone else has filled) worked better than
customer refilling [[src-loop-tesco-ends-trial]]. A pharmacy pack is pre-fill by
definition, which is the one variant Tesco found viable.

Algramo in Chile is the emerging-market case: RFID-tagged durable containers
refilled from in-store dispensers in Walmart and neighbourhood stores or from an
electric tricycle at the door, paid by the gram through an app, with a credit
each time the same container comes back. It runs through 2,000-plus
family-owned stores and rewards reuse with an 11.5% discount per refill
[[src-algramo-refill-wef]]. The transferable idea is the tagged container as
the unit of account: the customer's discount, the deposit and the hygiene log
all hang off a chip in the box. No SA grocer (Pick n Pay, Woolworths) was found
running a returnable tote or bag-return scheme on delivery; Woolworths' effort is
a reusable in-store bag ⚠️ UNVERIFIED as to any delivery return leg.

## Pharmacy take-back and recycling precedents

Pharmacy schemes split into two kinds, and neither reuses anything.

The first kind takes back empty packaging for material recycling. Boots piloted
in-store blister collection in 100-plus stores in February 2024 and expanded to
more than 800 UK stores in February 2025, paying 100 Advantage Card points per
five empty blisters; partner MYGroup separates foil from plastic and had
processed more than seven tonnes by early 2025 [[src-boots-blister-recycling]].
Superdrug's earlier TerraCycle scheme is the warning: adding every Superdrug
pharmacy in January 2021 produced "an encouragingly high uptake" that outran the
funding, and by May 2021 all independent pharmacies were dropped to keep the
collection viable [[src-superdrug-terracycle-blister]]. Australia's Pharmacycle
is the only scheme found that accepts emptied multi-dose adherence packs
(Webster-pak, SureMed) alongside manufacturer blisters, funded by a fee on the
participating pharmacy [[src-pharmacycle-blister-recycling]]. Chiesi's Take AIR
is the strongest evidence for a postal return loop through pharmacies: 20,049
inhalers posted back in pre-paid envelopes over twelve months, 62% of invited
pharmacies participating, and an estimated 119.3 tonnes CO2e avoided, in a
peer-reviewed evaluation [[src-chiesi-take-air-npj]]. GSK's nine-year Complete
the Cycle recovered over 2 million inhalers and still closed in 2020 because a
single-funder "bespoke, standalone scheme" could not reach scale
[[src-gsk-complete-the-cycle-closure]].

The second kind takes back unwanted medicine for destruction. Australia's
Commonwealth-funded RUM programme puts a bin in every pharmacy and incinerates
everything returned; its FAQ states that returned medicines are "in no way reused
or recycled" [[src-rum-natrum-australia]]. The US boards of pharmacy allow a
dispensed item back into stock only if it was out for delivery, came back
undelivered or refused, and never left the control of pharmacy staff or the
contracted courier [[src-nabp-return-reuse-position]]. The international norm
is consistent: a pack that has been in a patient's hands with medicine in it
goes to the disposal stream. That is why no scheme anywhere refills a
patient's used medicine container, and why a Dis-Chem returnable has to be the
box around the medicine, never the pack touching it.

## Sachet packaging materials and the waste question

Automated dose-sachet packagers (Parata, Synmed, Yuyama, RxSafe) run on rolls
of film sold as paper, foil or cellophane, and the common structures are
laminates of polyethylene with cellophane, polyester or foil
[[src-strip-packaging-film-materials]]. Laminates do not go through ordinary
film recycling; only mono-material (all-PE or all-PP) films do, and cellophane
is cellulose-based but not kerbside-recyclable in practice
[[src-strip-packaging-film-materials]]. PillPack's packets are PET, and Amazon
concedes that "some of our individual packets are recyclable while others
aren't" while it tests fully recyclable options [[src-pillpack-packaging-help]].
Any claim that Made-for-Me sachets are "recyclable" therefore depends on the
specific roll Dis-Chem's packager uses. ⚠️ UNVERIFIED for the Brix/ROWA line.

The comparison against manufacturer blisters cuts the other way. Blisters are
thin PVC (often with PVDC barrier) or cold-form aluminium bonded to foil
lidding, and the layers must be separated before either material is recovered,
which is why they are excluded from municipal recycling and need specialist
schemes like Boots' or Pharmacycle's [[src-blister-recyclability-review-2023]].
A sachet service that strips medicine out of blisters at the pharmacy moves
that waste upstream to a site that can aggregate it for a specialist recycler,
which is a real if unglamorous gain. On the dispenser itself, the only LCA found
compared single-use unit-dose packs with reusable medication dispensers in a
hospital setting: two of three reusable-dispenser scenarios carried a higher
burden than the unit-dose pack unless the dispenser was reused about 7.2 times
or more [[src-lca-unit-dose-dispenser-2023]]. A monthly returnable box that
survives eight or more cycles clears that bar; one that gets lost or damaged in
its first winter does not. No peer-reviewed study comparing sachet rolls with
original blister packs on a waste basis was found.

## Cold chain packaging

Two workable models exist, and no consumer pharmacy scheme was found that
collects cold packaging back through its own driver. The returnable model is
Walmart Specialty Pharmacy's Greenbox: an HDPE outer with reusable insulation
panels holding 2 to 8 degrees C for four days, reclaimed and cleaned at the
vendor's facilities, reused around 20 times on guarantee and 55 on average in
carrier circulation, and credited with a 50% shipping-cost saving
[[src-greenbox-walmart-specialty-reusable-shipper]]. The return leg used
FedEx/UPS, not a pharmacy van. The single-use model is the kerbside-recyclable
paper shipper: Amazon replaced plastic film, cotton fibre and mixed-plastic
insulation on its chilled grocery deliveries with a recycled-paper liner,
citing 15 million pounds of non-recyclable plastic avoided a year
[[src-amazon-recyclable-cold-packaging]], and Softbox's Tempcell ECO does the
same for pharma parcels in corrugated cardboard with a vendor-claimed hold of up
to 72 hours [[src-softbox-tempcell-eco]]. Amazon Pharmacy ships refrigerated
medicine separately in temperature-protective packaging and describes no
take-back [[src-pillpack-packaging-help]].

For Dis-Chem the returnable cool box is the cleaner fit, because the monthly
driver visit that swaps the dispenser box can swap the cool box too, and SAPC's
thermolabile standard already requires the 2 to 8 degree chain to be held to
the point of handover (see
[[wiki/dispensing-practice/inventory-coldchain-controlled]]). The constraint is
load-shedding at the customer's end, which no packaging solves: the box buys
hours, not days, and the handover has to be attended.

## What SA rules allow: returned medicine, GPP containers, EPR

Three rule sets bear on a returnable pack. SAPC's GPP standard on disposal and
destruction (Board Notice 100 of 2020) requires every medicine disposed of to be
recorded in the stock system, Schedule 5 and 6 quantities to be entered in the
register with witnesses, and destruction through a NEMWA-authorised facility
[[src-sapc-bn100-disposal-destruction]]. Medicine that comes back from a
patient enters that pathway. No SAPC sentence reading "returned medicine may not
be re-dispensed" could be retrieved, so the prohibition is inferred from the
disposal standard and from the identical rule in Australia and the US
[[src-rum-natrum-australia]] [[src-nabp-return-reuse-position]]; it is
⚠️ UNVERIFIED as a direct SA citation and logged in open questions. The GPP
Manual also carries sections on "Dispensing containers" and "Re-use of
containers" whose text could not be read [[src-sapc-bn100-disposal-destruction]];
until it is, assume the conservative reading that a container which has held a
patient's medicine is not refilled. A SA practice guide published in December
2024 found no readily available guidance on pharmaceutical waste for community
pharmacies and no national take-back scheme [[src-sapj-pharma-waste-disposal-2024]],
so a Dis-Chem returnable would be building the reverse stream from scratch.

The Extended Producer Responsibility Regulations (GN 1184 of 5 November 2020,
in force May 2021) make the brand owner the "producer" of packaging, and PRO
guidance treats a retailer as producer for its house brands; producers register
with DFFE, pay EPR fees per material and meet collection and recycling targets
[[src-epr-regulations-2020-gn1184]]. A Dis-Chem-branded dispenser box is
Dis-Chem's EPR obligation either way, and a reusable box that is counted,
returned and recycled at end of life is the kind of "circular economy
initiative" the regulations name as their purpose. The regulations help rather
than hinder.

## Design implications for Made-for-Me Medicine

Proven and legal now:

- A durable Dis-Chem dispenser box (the "sachet roll holder") owned by
  Dis-Chem, swapped by the driver at each monthly delivery, cleaned and
  re-issued. Precedent: Greenbox reuse cycles, Loop pre-fill, Sixty60 driver
  collection. It must reach eight or more cycles to beat single-use on an LCA
  basis [[src-lca-unit-dose-dispenser-2023]].
- A returnable cool box on the same swap for fridge lines, attended handover
  only, with the 2 to 8 degree chain logged to the door.
- Empty sachet film and stripped manufacturer blisters collected in the returned
  box and aggregated at the store for a specialist recycler, Boots- and
  Pharmacycle-style. This is where the real blister-waste win sits.
- A tagged box (RFID or QR) as the unit of deposit, hygiene log and loyalty
  credit, Algramo-style.

A stretch:

- Claiming the sachets themselves are recyclable or compostable. It depends on
  the film roll in the packager and is unverified for Dis-Chem's line.
- Customer-posted returns of anything (Take AIR worked with a manufacturer
  paying postage and a 12-month horizon; Complete the Cycle closed on scale).
- Funding: every packaging take-back that outgrew its sponsor (Superdrug, GSK)
  was cut back. Cost the return leg into the delivery model, not into a CSR
  budget.

🔮 requires-reform:

- Refilling any sachet, tray, bottle or blister that has held a patient's
  dispensed medicine. No jurisdiction found permits it, SAPC's disposal
  standard routes returns to destruction, and it would need a GPP rule change
  plus a hygiene and tamper-evidence regime that does not exist.
