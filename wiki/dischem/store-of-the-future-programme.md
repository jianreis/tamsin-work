# Store of the Future (SOTF) — programme overview

The hub page for **Store of the Future**, the Dis-Chem programme (McKinsey-run
delivery, TDC&Co store design, Bigly involvement on dispensary experience)
that redesigned the store around the **Better Health** ambition. The first
SOTF store opened at **Melrose Arch around May 2026** as an explicit
"test-and-learn pilot" and "proof point for future openings"
[[src-sotf-melrose-workshops-2026]]; by July 2026 a second site, **Irene**,
was running on the same processes [[src-sotf-training-launch-2026]]. Current
work evolves the live solutions from customer feedback and early results.
Unlike [[Home|Future of Scripting]], which designs a future state, SOTF is
operating today, workarounds and all.

## What SOTF is trying to achieve

SOTF is "the physical manifestation of Dis-Chem's brand aligning with the
Better Health ambition" [[src-sotf-steerco1-concept-2025]]. The parent
strategy ([[src-sotf-ecosystem-playback-2025]], Aug 2025) targets the ~50% of
South Africans who are poorly served or not served by private healthcare, and
answers five stated failures of SA healthcare: affordability, accessibility,
quality, transparency and service experience. The four ambitions Tamsin
relayed are confirmed by these documents and map to the strategy as follows:

1. **Integrated healthcare** - the ecosystem "flywheel": affordable health
   funding (Cover), a digital health platform, a curated provider network,
   and omnichannel retail/pharmacy assets, tied together by shared data.
2. **Increased access** - clinic-led primary care in store, nurse practising
   at top of license, virtual GP consults, TLC network reach.
3. **Reduced cost via Better Rewards** - loyalty is the declared integrator
   ("overall integration delivered through Better Rewards"): 10% base
   discount, pharmacy and Capitec boosts, cover-linked discounts.
4. **Better customer experience** - queue removal through ticketing,
   decoupled dispensing, a concierge Health Hub, experiential retail.

Eight success metrics were set at SteerCo #1, spanning category share, clinic
share, ecosystem cross-usage, cost-to-serve, digital adoption, Better Rewards
enrolment, innovation effectiveness and social impact
[[src-sotf-steerco1-concept-2025]]. ⚠️ No measured results against these
metrics have reached the vault yet.

## The key design decisions, and why they were made

The store is anchored on the **"T"**: Cover, Pharmacy (dispensary) and Clinic
across the back wall, the Health Hub and innovation aisle down the centre,
front-shop retail either side. The stated rationales are in the SteerCo pack;
they are commercial and operational, not customer-cited
[[src-sotf-steerco1-concept-2025]] [[src-sotf-steerco-decisions-2025]].

| Area | Decision | Stated rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Dispensary | Pharmacy stays at the back of the store | "Maintain the T concept and drive customers to the store" |
| Dispensary | Decoupled script submission and collection (separate counters) | Roaming time for customers ("drives front-shop traffic"); consolidated pick-and-pack efficiency |
| Dispensary | End-to-end digital ticketing (Qmatic), digital or printed | Queue removal; status visible on screens/app/WhatsApp |
| Dispensary | Unified OTC + script flow | Less space and resourcing; single operational flow |
| Dispensary | Lockers deprioritised | "Challenging economics and limited evidence of successful 24/7 pharmacy" |
| Clinic | Nurse-led model with Clinic Care Assistant pre-consult; admin moved to Hub | Nurses "practice at top of license"; frees consultation time |
| Clinic | E-script sent electronically clinic → dispensary | Ecosystem integration; medication ready within minutes |
| Cover | Financial advisor always in-store, dedicated room; triggers across all assets | In-person advisor "as focus due to conversion rate" |
| Hub | Central Health Hub desk as the single front door and reception for everything | "Conversion engine for ecosystem adoption"; protects nurse/pharmacist time |
| Front shop | Category captainship strategy; ~R455m of non-health FMCG exited | Align range to Better Health; captainship = 53% of revenue on 34% of space |
| Retail | Front-shop click & collect NOT built | "Until customer need is validated during relaunch of e-commerce" |

What launched at Melrose vs what was deferred: payment at the dispensary
(2028), in-store digital script submission (H2 2026), store navigation,
lockers, ESL/personalised pricing, AR skin analysis and gamified experiential
screens all slipped past MVP; the ticketing spine, decoupled counters,
clinic-to-dispensary e-script, Cover room and Hub roles went live
[[src-sotf-melrose-workshops-2026]] [[src-sotf-training-launch-2026]].

## Were the decisions research-informed? (assessment)

Tamsin's hypothesis was that SOTF decisions were made by business rather than
informed by customer research. The documents largely bear that out, with
nuance:

- The concept was **validated by SteerCo (7 Nov 2025) before any dedicated
  customer research on it existed**. Customer needs in the SteerCo pack come
  from four workshop-defined personas (Nandi, Lerato, Andrea, Thabo), not a
  cited study; the one research reference is an unattributed line ("Consumer
  research notes high appetite for digital integration"); digital innovations
  were prioritised from "expert interviews, desk research, market reports"
  [[src-sotf-steerco1-concept-2025]].
- Rationales throughout are operational and commercial. The internal pre-read
  even words the dispensary-location principle as "drive cross-sell", softened
  to "drive customers towards front shop" in the shared version.
- The Jan 2026 interview study [[src-dc-store-future-interviews-2026]] was a
  **pre-launch concept test of already-made decisions**, and the Feb 2026
  workshops that locked the Melrose MVP do not cite it
  [[src-sotf-melrose-workshops-2026]]. Research arrived as reassurance, not
  as input.
- To be fair to the programme: several business-led decisions **converge**
  with what customers later said they wanted - ticketing was the strongest
  interview consensus (11/12), phone notifications and roam-while-waiting
  match the "step away without losing my place" need, and the three-call
  pharmacy rule is a graduated recovery mechanism.
- But there are **live tensions** with the customer evidence worth testing
  against post-launch feedback:
  - The 10-minute late rule cancels clinic/cover bookings, and "walk-in" was
    eliminated as an operating concept - against 9/12 customers saying a
    missed turn "should not mean starting over" and wanting grace and staff
    discretion.
  - Payment stayed at front-shop POS (dispensary payment deferred to 2028),
    so the pay-in-the-front-queue pain the ticketing was meant to remove
    survives at the end of the journey.
  - No direct WhatsApp number for the store, with calls centralised and
    primarily outbound - against the phone-first support preference.
  - Digital-only failure modes (missed digital ticket, wrong mobile number)
    already fill the unhappy-journey catalogues, echoing the interviews'
    "digital must keep a visible human fallback".

## Where the live solution is already straining (evolution targets)

The training pack documents workarounds in production
[[src-sotf-training-launch-2026]]: a manual VIP "A-ticket" flow for
non-integrated online collections (handwritten ticket numbers, manual Qmatic
closure), manual suffix-linked tickets for one collector with many scripts
(care facilities), H-ticket fallback queues for failed digital journeys, and
long unhappy-journey catalogues across Hub, pharmacy and clinic. These, plus
the deferred MVP items, are the natural candidate list for the evolution work.

## Open questions

- ⚠️ **Early results are still absent**: no trading, adoption, NPS, queue-time
  or clinic-utilisation data against the eight success metrics has been
  ingested.
- ⚠️ **Post-launch customer feedback is still absent**: the only customer
  evidence remains the pre-launch n=12 study. Live behaviour vs concept-test
  sentiment (especially on the late rule, POS payment and digital fallbacks)
  is unmeasured in the vault.
- Which SOTF solution is Tamsin's evolution project targeting, and against
  which feedback?
- No delivery-process training document is held (the supplied file duplicated
  the online-collections content) - the delivery leg of the live journey is
  undocumented here.
- How is Irene performing relative to Melrose Arch, and what changed between
  the two openings?
